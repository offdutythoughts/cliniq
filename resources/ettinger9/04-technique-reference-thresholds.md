### Ch 73 — Point-of-Care Ultrasound Examination (POCUS / AFAST / TFAST)

#### AFAST Abdominal Fluid Scoring System (AFSS)

| Score threshold | Fluid dimension cutoff | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| "Weak positive" (score ½) | fluid pocket dimension at or below threshold | Minimal free fluid |
| "Strong positive" (score 1) | fluid pocket dimension above threshold | Definite free fluid |
| Cutoff: cats | 5 mm maximum fluid dimension | below = weak; above = strong |
| Cutoff: dogs | 1 cm (10 mm) maximum fluid dimension | below = weak; above = strong |
| Total AFSS score <3 | — | Small-volume bleeder/effusion |
| Total AFSS score ≥3 | — | Large-volume bleeder/effusion |

*Note: HR5th view scored the same way but not included in the total AFSS score. Score guides transfusion need, surgical intervention, and fluid resuscitation decisions.*

#### CVC (Caudal Vena Cava) Respirophasic Height Change — Volume Status

| Category | Height change (respirophasic) | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Fluid responsive ("bounce") | 35–50% change | Volume-responsive |
| Fluid intolerant ("FAT") | <10% change + increased maximum height | Right-sided fluid overload / CHF |
| Fluid starved / hypovolemic ("flat") | <10% change + decreased maximum height | Hypovolemia |

*Hepatic venous distension ("tree trunk sign") has high sensitivity and specificity for increased right-sided filling pressures in dogs and cats in lateral, standing, or sternal position.*

#### Vet BLUE B-Line Scoring System

| Score | Category | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| 1, 2, 3 B-lines | Weak positive | Alveolar-interstitial edema possible |
| >3 B-lines or "infinity" | Strong positive | Clinically significant wet lung |
| 0 B-lines (A-lines + lung sliding) | Dry lung / normal | Rules out clinically relevant left-sided CHF |

#### TFAST PTX Lung Point 1/3s Rule

| Lung point location | Severity |
|---|---|
| Upper 1/3 of thorax | Trivial to mild PTX |
| Middle 1/3 of thorax | Moderate PTX |
| Lower 1/3 of thorax | Severe PTX |

#### Free Air Detection

Ultrasound sensitivity: volumes as small as **0.2 mL** of free air in the peritoneal cavity/retroperitoneal space can be detected.

#### Urinary Bladder Volume Estimation (AFAST CC view)

Formula: **L × H × W (cm) × 0.625 = estimated urinary bladder volume (mL)**

### Ch 78 — Glucose Monitoring

*This chapter addresses PBGM device accuracy and CGMS technology. Target glucose curves and nadir goals for diabetic management are addressed in Ch 291 (canine DM) and Ch 292 (feline DM), not in this techniques chapter. No diabetes management numeric targets (nadir, curve goal) appear here.*

#### PBGM Device Accuracy Standards (ISO 15197:2013 — human standard applied as reference)

| BG concentration range | Required accuracy |
|---|---|
| <100 mg/dL (5.6 mmol/L) | ≥95% of readings within ±15 mg/dL (0.84 mmol/L) of reference |
| ≥100 mg/dL (5.6 mmol/L) | ≥95% of readings within ±15% of reference |
| All ranges | 99% of readings must fall within Parkes EGA zones A + B |

#### AlphaTRAK PBGM Performance in Cats (reported accuracy)

| Condition | Value |
|---|---|
| Measurement range (AlphaTRAK) | 20–750 mg/dL (1.1–41.7 mmol/L) |
| Blood volume required | 0.3 mcL |
| Accuracy at BGc <100 mg/dL | 90% of values within ±15 mg/dL (0.83 mmol/L) of reference |
| Overestimation (cats): median difference | +4.4 mg/dL (0.24 mmol/L) range 1.8–72 mg/dL (0.1–4 mmol/L) |
| Underestimation (cats): median difference | −12.6 mg/dL (−0.7 mmol/L) range −1.8 to −122 mg/dL |

#### FreeStyle Libre FGMS (Flash Glucose Monitoring System)

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Sensor detection limits | 20–500 mg/dL (1.1–27.8 mmol/L); outside = "LO"/"HI" |
| ISF-to-BG lag time (dogs) | 5–12 minutes |
| ISF-to-BG lag time (cats) | 11–30 minutes |
| Sensor lifespan (dogs) | Up to 14 days |
| Sensor lifespan (cats) | 5.5–8 days (reduced due to skin/adherence issues) |
| Mean accuracy vs hexokinase reference (dogs with DM) | 2.3 ± 46.8 mg/dL (0.1 ± 2.6 mmol/L) |
| Accuracy note (dogs) | Less accurate for BG <90 mg/dL (5 mmol/L); affected by skin thickness <5 mm |
| Accuracy (cats) | Relative deviation from hexokinase: 9.96%; slightly higher in hypo/normoglycemic range (12.3% and 12.6%) |

#### Traditional CGMS Recording Range

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Recording range (traditional CGMS requiring calibration) | 40–400 mg/dL (2.2–22 mmol/L) |
| Calibration frequency | 2–3 times/day required |

#### RBC Glucose Distribution (species difference — source of PBGM error)

| Species | Glucose within RBCs (% of total) |
|---|---|
| Dogs | ~12.5% |
| Cats | ~7% |
| Humans | ~50% |

*Because dog/cat RBCs contain far less glucose than human RBCs, human plasma-calibrated PBGMs systematically underestimate BGc in dogs and cats.*

#### Whole Blood Glucose Decay In Vitro

| Condition | Rate |
|---|---|
| Unseparated whole blood (room temp) | ~5–10% per hour decrease in BG |

### Ch 79 — Buccal Mucosal Bleeding Time (BMBT)

| Species | Published reference interval | Clinical "normal" cutoff |
|---|---|---|
| Dogs | 1.8–4.7 minutes | <4 minutes |
| Cats | 0.5–2.5 minutes | <3 minutes |
| Pathologic prolongation (thrombopathia, thrombasthenia, type II/III vWD dogs; Chédiak-Higashi cats) | >10 minutes | — |

*Device specification: standardized automated spring-loaded device creating a 5 mm long × 1 mm deep incision. Non-standard devices (e.g., Surgicutt Junior, scalpel blade) produce different bleeding times and inaccurate results.*

*Thrombocytopenic patients (<70,000 platelets/mcL): BMBT provides no additional information (prolonged by default).*

### Ch 87 — Arthrocentesis

#### Synovial Fluid Cytologic Reference Values (Table 87.1)

| Condition | Color | Turbidity | Viscosity | Total Protein (g/dL) | Nucleated Cells (cells/mcL) | Mono (%) | PMN (%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Normal | Colorless | Clear | High | <2.5 | <3,000 | >95 | <5 |
| Degenerative joint disease | Colorless | Clear | Variable | <2.5 | <5,000 | >90 | <10 |
| Trauma | Colorless to yellow | Hazy | Decreased | Variable | 2,500–3,000 | >75 | <25 |
| Infectious (bacterial, Ehrlichia) | Yellow to bloody | Cloudy | Decreased | >2.5 | 40,000–250,000 | <10 | >90 |
| Immune-mediated (idiopathic, rheumatoid, erosive) | Yellow | Hazy | Decreased | >2.5 | 4,400–350,000 | 20–80 | 20–80 |

*Unit conversion: total protein g/dL × 10 = g/L (e.g., <2.5 g/dL = <25 g/L).*
*Mono = monocytes; PMN = polymorphonuclear cells.*

#### Synovial Fluid Viscosity

Normal joint fluid strings **3–5 cm** between thumb and index finger. Abnormal (inflammatory) joint fluid strings shorter lengths due to decreased hyaluronan.

#### Volume

- Normal joint: may yield only **≤0.2 mL**; apparent absence of fluid may indicate a healthy joint.
- Abnormal joints: sometimes **0.5–1 mL** easily aspirated.

### Ch 91 — Blood Pressure Measurement

#### ACVIM 2018 Consensus Classification of Systolic Arterial Pressure (SAP)

*Applies to both dogs and cats, using appropriate equipment under recommended conditions.*

| SAP (mm Hg) | Classification | Risk of Target Organ Damage (TOD) |
|---|---|---|
| <140 | Normotensive | Minimal |
| 140–159 | Prehypertensive | Low |
| 160–179 | Hypertensive | Moderate |
| ≥180 | Severely hypertensive | High |

#### Technical Standards

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Number of consecutive measurements | 5–7 consecutive and consistent readings |
| Acceptable variability between readings | <20% |
| First measurement | Discard |
| Cuff width | 30–40% of limb/tail circumference |
| Inflatable portion length | ~80% of limb circumference |
| Acclimatization time before measuring | Minimum 5–10 minutes |
| Equipment calibration interval | Every 6 months |
| Doppler cuff inflation above occlusion | 20–40 mm Hg above the point where blood flow is no longer heard |

#### Notes on Method

- **Doppler**: measures SAP only; DAP assessment unreliable (wide interindividual variation).
- **Oscillometric / HDO**: provides SAP, DAP, MAP; HDO may be preferable in cats and small dogs.
- **Current guidelines focus on SAP** for hypertension classification, as most hypertensive dogs/cats display systolic hypertension.
- No NIBP device has been fully validated (per AHCP-VBPS criteria) for use in conscious dogs or cats as of this edition.
- "White coat" / situational hypertension is a recognized confounder; decisions should be based on ≥2 occasions in most cases without TOD.

### Ch 96 — Electrocardiography

#### Normal ECG Reference Ranges (Table 96.2)

*Recorded in right lateral recumbency at 25 or 50 mm/sec; 10 mm/mV standard.*

| Parameter | Dogs | Cats |
|---|---|---|
| Heart rate (bpm) | 70–160 (adult); 60–140 (giant breeds); 80–180 (toy breeds); up to 220 (puppies) | 140–240 (in-hospital); 100–120 (home environment) |
| P wave amplitude (mV) | ≤0.4 | ≤0.2 |
| P wave duration (msec) | <40 (up to 50 in giant breeds) | <40 |
| P-R interval (msec) | 60–130 (varies inversely with heart rate) | 50–90 (varies inversely with heart rate) |
| R wave amplitude (mV) | 0.5–2.5 or 3.0 in leads II, III, aVF; <3 mV in V10 (V6); <5 mV in V2 and V4 | <0.9 |
| QRS duration (msec) | <60 | <40 |
| Q-T interval (msec) | 150–250 (varies inversely with heart rate) | 70–200 (varies inversely with heart rate) |
| ST segment (mV) | <0.2 mV depression in limb leads; <0.25 mV depression in precordial leads; <0.15 mV elevation in leads II and III; coving or slurring of ST segment may indicate abnormal repolarization | No depression or elevation |
| T wave amplitude | <25% of R wave amplitude; positive in lead V1 and negative in V10 (V4); positive in V10 in Chihuahuas | Usually positive; amplitude <0.3 mV |
| Mean Electrical Axis (MEA) | +40° to +100° | 0° to ±180° |

#### ECG Filter Settings

| Species | Recommended low-pass filter cutoff |
|---|---|
| Dogs | 50–70 Hz |
| Cats | 150 Hz |

#### Holter / Ambulatory Monitor

- Holter: records continuously for **24 or 48 consecutive hours**.
- External cardiac event monitor: battery life usually **5–7 days**.
- ILR (implantable loop recorder): diagnostic yield **48–58%** in dogs with intermittent weakness/syncope.

#### TAPSE (Tricuspid Annular Plane Systolic Excursion) — RV Systolic Dysfunction

| Method | Dogs: cutoff for RV dysfunction | Cats |
|---|---|---|
| MM TAPSE, normalized | <4.8 mm/kg^0.3 | Insufficient data — assess subjectively |
| 2DE TAPSE, normalized | <3.2 mm/kg^0.3 | Insufficient data |
| MM TAPSE/Ao_Sx | <0.65 | — |

#### Subjective RV Size Criteria

- RV wall thickness normally appears **one-third to one-half** that of the LV.
- RV minor dimension normally **<50%** of LV minor dimension.
- Normal RA: appears at least slightly smaller than LA; never larger than LA.

#### E-Point to Septal Separation (EPSS)

The E-point to IVS vertical distance (from M-mode mitral valve level) is an inverse correlate of ejection fraction; has diagnostic relevance in dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM). No specific numeric cutoff is stated in this chapter for normal vs. abnormal; the chapter cites Holler & Wess (2014, JVIM) for DCM thresholds in Doberman Pinschers.

#### Transmitral E-Wave Velocity — Clinical Thresholds in Dogs with MMVD

| E-wave velocity | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| >1.0 m/s (dogs with left-sided cardiomegaly) | Increased risk for heart failure or cardiac death |
| >1.2–1.4 m/s (dogs with MMVD) | Associated with reduced survival |

#### Bernoulli Equation — Pressure Gradient Estimation

Formula: **PG (mm Hg) = 4 × (velocity [m/s])^2**

Normal PA pressure assumed: ~**25 mm Hg** (used as baseline for RV systolic pressure estimation from transpulmonary gradient).

RA pressure for TV regurgitation calculation: **0–5 mm Hg** (negligible) or at most **10–15 mm Hg**.

#### Doppler Angle of Incidence

Must be **<20°** for accurate Doppler velocity measurement.

#### Tissue Doppler Imaging (TDI) Velocity Range

Normal myocardial tissue velocities: **<20 cm/s** (lower than blood flow velocities).

#### Pulmonary Hypertension — TR Velocity for Diagnosis

TV regurgitation velocity interrogated with CWD provides indirect estimate of systolic PA pressure. Elevated systolic PA pressure and TR PG are key metrics for diagnosis and severity assessment of pulmonary hypertension (ch. 218).

#### Normal Aortic / Pulmonary Flow Velocities

*No explicit normal Doppler velocity table for aortic or pulmonary outflow in this chapter. The chapter notes that blood flow velocities commonly exceed 100 cm/s (especially in outflow tracts), causing aliasing on CDI. Cutoffs for stenosis severity are referenced to ch. 231.*

#### Protein Electrophoresis of Cisternal CSF (% fractions)

| Fraction | Dogs | Cats |
|---|---|---|
| Albumin | 34–53% | 1–53% |
| Alpha globulin | 12–32% | 0–48% |
| Beta globulin | 18–31% | 46–95% |
| Gamma globulin | 2–9% | 0–29% |

#### Differential Cell Count

Normal differential: predominantly **lymphocytes and monocytes**; few (<20%) non-degenerate neutrophils; rare erythrocytes.

#### Unit Conversion Notes

- Total protein: mg/dL × 0.01 = g/L (e.g., 20 mg/dL = 0.2 g/L); or mg/dL × 10 = mg/L.
- Glucose: mg/dL × 0.056 = mmol/L (e.g., 100 mg/dL = 5.6 mmol/L).

#### CSF Collection Volume

Safe limit: approximately **1 mL per 5 kg** body weight.

#### Sample Stability

CSF should be processed within **4 hours** of collection; if delayed, addition of autologous serum (100 mcL serum/1 mL CSF) or 6% hydroxyethyl starch (1:1 vol:vol) may preserve cellular constituents for up to **48 hours**.

#### Myelography — Contrast Agent Dosing

- Non-ionic agents: iohexol or iopamidol, **180–300 mg iodine/mL**.
- Volume: **0.3–0.45 mL/kg** body weight (site-dependent).
- Post-myelographic seizure rate in dogs: **3–21%**.

#### Lumbar Puncture Sites

- Dogs: L5–L6 (most common); L4–L5 alternative.
- Cats: L6–L7.
