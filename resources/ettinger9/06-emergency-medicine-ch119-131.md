### Ch 119 — Triage and Initial Stabilization

> ClinIQ: no dedicated triage protocol (candidate — general stabilization content supports all PROT-* entries)

#### Key Definitions
- **Triage**: sorting of patients based on medical priority when resources are insufficient; sickest treated first.
- **Veterinary Triage List (VTL)**: 5-level system (levels 1–5); levels 1–2 require immediate attention.
- **Shock Index (SI)**: HR/systolic BP; SI >0.9 in dogs associated with hemodynamic instability.

#### Initial Assessment Framework (ABCDE)
1. **Airway** — stridor/stertor, inspiratory effort, SpO₂; interventions: O₂, IV catheter, orotracheal intubation, sedation, cooling
2. **Breathing** — RR, effort, thoracic auscultation, chest/diaphragm movement; interventions: O₂, medications (bronchodilators, diuretics)
3. **Circulation** — HR, mucous membrane color, CRT, pulse quality, BP, extremity temperature, rectal-interdigital temp gradient
4. **Disability (Neurologic)** — mentation (AVPU or modified Glasgow Coma Scale), reflexes
5. **Exposure** — full body exam

#### Shock Recognition (Physical Exam Targets)
- Tachycardia (dogs) or tachycardia/bradycardia (cats) — species-specific interpretation required
- Pale/white mucous membranes, CRT >2 sec
- Weak/absent peripheral pulses
- Cool extremities; rectal-interdigital temperature gradient (>4°C = shock marker in dogs)
- Altered mentation (obtundation, coma)
- Hypotension (Doppler systolic <80 mmHg dog, <60 mmHg cat = severe)

#### Lactate Thresholds
- Elevated lactate (>2 mmol/L) = tissue hypoperfusion; serial monitoring for treatment response
- Lactate >4 mmol/L = high mortality risk

#### Modified Glasgow Coma Scale (MGCS) — TBI prognosis
- Score 3–8: poor prognosis
- Score 9–14: guarded prognosis
- Score 15–18: good prognosis
- ~50% of patients scoring 8 survive; survivors mean 14, non-survivors mean 9

#### System-Specific Emergency Interventions (from triage)
- **Respiratory distress**: O₂ supplementation; orotracheal intubation for non-responsive/no gag reflex; sedation for restraint
- **Cardiovascular shock**: IV catheter placement; fluid bolus (see Ch 121); BP monitoring
- **Neurologic (suspected brain herniation)**: orotracheal intubation + mechanical ventilation to maintain PaCO₂ 30–40 mmHg; avoid jugular compression; elevate head ~30 degrees
- **Seizures**: benzodiazepine IV/IM/IN as first-line; PoC bloodwork for glucose and electrolytes
- **Urinary obstruction (male cat)**: ECG for hyperkalemia; IV catheter; urethralization; emergency serum K+/creatinine
- **Hypoglycemia**: dextrose supplementation (see Ch 61)
- **Toxin ingestion**: decontamination if no/few signs (see Ch 132)

### Ch 120 — Fluid Therapy

> ClinIQ: no dedicated fluid therapy protocol (candidate gap); content supports PROT-SHOCK, PROT-SEIZ

#### Body Fluid Compartments
| Compartment | % Body Weight | % Total Body Water |
|---|---|---|
| Intracellular | 40% | 8/12 (2/3) |
| Interstitial | 15% | 3/12 (1/4) |
| Intravascular | 5% | 1/12 |
| Transcellular | 1% | 1/50 |
| Total body water | 60% | — |

#### Dehydration Assessment (TABLE 120.1)
| % Dehydration | Clinical Signs |
|---|---|
| <5% | Often none; history of vomiting/diarrhea; ± "doughy abdomen" |
| 5% | Tacky mucous membranes; mild skin tent |
| 6–8% | Skin tent 2–3 sec; dry mucous membranes; mild tachycardia |
| 10–12% | Prolonged skin tent; sunken eyes; marked tachycardia; weak pulse |
| >12% | All above + shock signs; death imminent without treatment |

#### Crystalloid Fluid Compositions (TABLE 120.3 — key fluids)
| Fluid | Na (mEq/L) | Cl (mEq/L) | K (mEq/L) | Lactate/Buffer | pH | Osmolarity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Plasma-Lyte A | 140 | 98 | 5 | Acetate 27 + Gluconate 23 | 7.4 | 294 |
| Normosol-R | 140 | 98 | 5 | Acetate 27 + Gluconate 23 | 6.4 | 295 |
| Lactated Ringer's | 130 | 109 | 4 | Lactate 28 | 6.5 | 273 |
| 0.9% NaCl | 154 | 154 | 0 | None | 5.0 | 308 |
| 7% NaCl (hypertonic) | 1197 | 1197 | 0 | None | — | ~2400 |

#### Potassium Supplementation (TABLE 120.2)
| Supplementation Rate (mEq/kg/hr) | Serum K (mEq/L) | Add (mEq) to 1 L crystalloid | Max Fluid Rate (mL/kg/hr) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.1 | 3.5–5 | 20 | 25 |
| 0.2 | 3.1–3.5 | 28 | 18 |
| 0.3 | 2.6–3.0 | 40 | 12 |
| 0.4 | 2–2.5 | 60 | 8 |
| 0.5 | <2 | 80 | 6 |
- **Maximum K administration rate: never exceed 0.5 mEq/kg/hr** (risk of cardiac dysrhythmia)

#### Albumin Replacement Formula
- ALB deficit (g) = 10 × [desired ALB (g/dL) − patient ALB (g/dL)] × body weight (kg) × 0.3
- Plasma: 22.5 mL/kg provides 0.5 g/dL albumin

#### Fluid Volume Strategy
- **Shock resuscitation**: aliquots of 20–25% of one blood volume (90 mL/kg dogs, 50 mL/kg cats); administer over 10–20 min; reassess after each bolus
- **Hypertonic saline**: 7–7.5% at 4–6 mL/kg over 10–20 min (concurrent TBI, hypovolemia without dehydration)
- **Sepsis crystalloid boluses**: dogs 15–20 mL/kg, cats 10–15 mL/kg (see Ch 128)
- **Colloid boluses**: dogs 5–10 mL/kg, cats 2–5 mL/kg, IV to effect
- **Avoid full "shock dose"** (dogs 90 mL/kg, cats 50 mL/kg) in sepsis — overhydration worsens outcomes
- Fluid overload risk if body weight increases >10% from baseline
- ROSE strategy: Resuscitation → Optimization → Stabilization → Evacuation

#### 0.9% Saline Caution
- Promotes hyperchloremic metabolic acidosis; reduces renal blood flow/GFR; associated with increased mortality in people; prefer balanced crystalloids (Plasma-Lyte, LRS)

### Ch 121 — Circulatory Shock: Identification and Management

> ClinIQ: ~ PROT-SHOCK

#### Shock Classification (TABLE 121.1)
| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| Hypovolemic | Hemorrhage, severe vomiting/diarrhea, polyuria, burns, third-spacing |
| Distributive | Anaphylaxis, neurogenic, mast cell degranulation, sepsis/SIRS, drugs/toxins, hypoadrenocorticism |
| Cardiogenic | DCM, myocarditis, end-stage MVD, feline cardiomyopathies, arrhythmias, cardiotoxins |
| Metabolic | Cyanide, mitochondrial dysfunction, hypoglycemia, severe hypoxia/anemia, CO/methemoglobin |
| Obstructive | Thromboembolic disease, cardiac tamponade, GDV |

#### Physical Exam Findings in Shock (TABLE 121.2)
| Sign | Compensated Vasoconstrictive | Decompensated | Vasodilatory |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mentation | Dull | Obtundation | Variable |
| MM color | Pale | Pale-white | Hyperemic (brick red) |
| CRT | Normal to prolonged | Prolonged | Rapid |
| Pulse quality | Adequate to weak | Weak to absent | Hyperdynamic |
| Temperature | Hypothermia | Profound hypothermia | Warm, fever |
| HR (dog) | Tachycardia | Tachycardia | Tachycardia |
| HR (cat) | Tachycardia OR bradycardia | Tachycardia or bradycardia | Tachycardia |

#### Oxygen Therapy
- All pets in shock should initially receive O₂ supplementation; target PaO₂ >80 mmHg (SpO₂ >94%)
- Avoid prolonged O₂ in non-hypoxemic patients (risk of O₂ toxicosis)

#### Volume Resuscitation
- Aliquots of isotonic crystalloid: **20–25% of one blood volume** (dog blood vol ~90 mL/kg; cat ~50 mL/kg) → each bolus = ~20–25 mL/kg dog, ~10–15 mL/kg cat, IV over 10–20 min; reassess after each bolus
- **Hypertonic saline**: 7–7.5% at **4–6 mL/kg IV over 10–20 min** — for hypovolemic shock, esp. concurrent TBI; not for dehydrated or hypernatremic patients
- **Hemorrhagic shock**: hypotensive resuscitation strategy may be appropriate (Ch 124)
- Transfusion therapy: fresh whole blood, pRBCs, FFP for significant hemorrhage
- Hemoglobin-based oxygen carriers (if available) for RBC unavailability/incompatibility

#### Vasopressor and Inotropic Agents (TABLE 121.3)
| Agent | Mechanism | Dose (IV CRI) | Indications |
|---|---|---|---|
| Norepinephrine | Alpha-1 + mild beta-1 | **0.05–2 mcg/kg/min** | First-line septic shock (human guidelines); vasodilatory shock |
| Epinephrine | Alpha + beta | **0.05–1 mcg/kg/min** | Anaphylaxis; second-line sepsis; decreased contractility + hypotension |
| Dopamine | Beta (low dose) + alpha (high dose) | **5–20 mcg/kg/min** | ± First-line sepsis; feline cardiogenic shock |
| Phenylephrine | Alpha-1 | **0.5–5 mcg/kg/min** | Hypotension + inappropriately elevated HR + normal contractility |
| Dobutamine | Beta-1 | **5–20 mcg/kg/min** | Hypotension + decreased contractility in cardiogenic shock |
| Pimobendan | Calcium sensitization + PDE-III inhibition | **0.25 mg/kg PO q8–12h** | Decreased contractility with cardiogenic shock |
| Hydrocortisone | Potentiates catecholamines | **0.25–1 mg/kg q6h or 0.1–0.16 mg/kg/h IV** | Refractory vasopressor-dependent septic shock |
| Vasopressin | Vasopressin-1 agonism | **0.5–5 mU/kg/min** | Second-line sepsis (human guidelines) |

*All CRI unless stated otherwise.*
- Do NOT use sympathomimetics in hypovolemic patients until volume replaced
- Dobutamine **contraindicated in cats with HCM**
- Persistent hypotension despite volume deficit correction → vasopressor/inotrope

### Ch 122 — Diagnostic Testing for the Emergency Patient

> ClinIQ: no dedicated protocol; content supports all PROT-* entries (diagnostic workup)

#### Point-of-Care (PoC) Priorities (all emergency patients)
- **Blood glucose**: rule out hypoglycemia in seizure, altered mentation, critical illness
- **PCV/TP**: assess anemia and protein; PCV <20% (dog), <15% (cat) = transfusion consideration
- **Blood smear**: thrombocytopenia (platelet clumps before diagnosing), cell morphology
- **Blood gas (arterial or venous)**: pH, PCO₂, HCO₃⁻, lactate, base excess; acid-base disturbance classification
- **Electrolytes**: Na, K, Ca, Cl; hyperkalemia in urethral obstruction/AKI
- **BUN/creatinine**: azotemia screen
- **Coagulation (PT/aPTT)**: PT extrinsic/common pathway (VII, X, V, II); aPTT intrinsic/common (XII, XI, IX, VIII, X, V, II)
- **Blood pressure**: Doppler (most accurate in cats); non-invasive NIBP; target systolic >90 mmHg
- **Pulse oximetry**: SpO₂ >95% = adequate; less reliable in low-flow states; more accurate in anesthetized patients
- **Urinalysis**: specific gravity, glucose, protein, sediment
- **PoC ultrasound (AFAST/TFAST)**: free fluid detection, cardiac function, pleural space

#### Acid-Base Interpretation Algorithm
- pH <7.35 = acidemia → check PCO₂ (↑ = respiratory acidosis) vs HCO₃⁻ (↓ = metabolic acidosis)
- pH >7.45 = alkalemia → check PCO₂ (↓ = respiratory alkalosis) vs HCO₃⁻ (↑ = metabolic alkalosis)
- Assess compensation; if absent or disproportionate → mixed disorder

#### Coagulation Interpretation
- PT prolonged + aPTT normal: extrinsic pathway defect (factor VII)
- PT normal + aPTT prolonged: intrinsic pathway defect (hemophilia A, Hageman trait)
- Both prolonged: throughout cascade; PT > aPTT excess = vitamin K deficiency (anticoagulant rodenticide, hepatobiliary disease)
- Mild prolongation (<120% normal on PoC) may be clinically inconsequential

#### Lactate
- >2 mmol/L: hypoperfusion; serial monitoring guides treatment
- Peritoneal fluid glucose >20 mg/dL below blood glucose = diagnostic for septic effusion

### Ch 123 — Respiratory Crisis

> ClinIQ: ~ PROT-RESP, PROT-THOR

#### Key Decision Framework
1. Immediate O₂ supplementation (flow-by, mask, oxygen cage)
2. If cannot oxygenate: orotracheal intubation; if cannot intubate → retrograde wire technique or **emergent tracheostomy**
3. After stabilization: thoracic radiography, echocardiography, cytology as indicated

#### PoC NT-proBNP
- Useful to differentiate cardiac vs non-cardiac dyspnea if imaging not immediately available

#### Common Pitfalls
- **Bronchodilators in dogs**: rarely useful — dogs do not have naturally occurring bronchoconstriction
- **Furosemide**: do NOT give routinely to all respiratory distress cases; identify source first; O₂, cough suppressants, anti-inflammatory glucocorticoids may have greater utility in dogs
- **Pulse oximetry**: limited value awake in dogs; more accurate in anesthetized dogs/cats
- **US**: valuable but do not use in place of therapeutics or examination

#### Ventilation Targets (for intubated patients)
- Target PaCO₂ 30–40 mmHg (TBI patients)
- PaCO₂ <30 mmHg → vasoconstriction and ischemia
- PaCO₂ >45 mmHg → vasodilation and elevated ICP

#### Respiratory Monitoring
- Serial respiratory rate and effort most useful
- Arterial blood gas for definitive evaluation
- PoC US for pleural recurrence or volume depletion

#### Airway Emergency Algorithm
1. Flow-by O₂
2. Intubation attempts (laryngoscope, lateral recumbency facilitates)
3. If failure: retrograde: large-bore catheter/needle through trachea → guide wire through larynx → ET tube over wire
4. Last resort: emergent tracheostomy

### Ch 124 — Bleeding Crisis

> ClinIQ: no dedicated bleeding protocol (candidate gap); PROT-SHOCK relevant for fluid/transfusion

#### Initial Approach
- Stabilize cardiovascular system before in-depth diagnostics
- Collect citrate + EDTA tubes **before** any fluid/blood product administration (avoid obscuring results)
- Avoid jugular sampling

#### Hemostatic Disorders — Primary vs Secondary
| Sign | Primary Hemostatic Defect | Secondary Hemostatic Defect | Hyperfibrinolysis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Petechiae | Yes | No | No |
| Ecchymoses | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Hemarthrosis | No | Yes | No |
| Hematomas | Rare | Yes | Yes |
| Hemoperitoneum | Uncommon | Yes | Yes |
| Hemothorax | Uncommon | Yes | Yes |

#### Transfusion Triggers
- Platelet count <50 × 10³/mcL = associated with spontaneous bleeding; check for platelet clumps before diagnosing thrombocytopenia
- PCV <20% dog, <15% cat = transfusion consideration; earlier transfusion triggers in sepsis

#### Blood Typing — Dog
- DEA system: DEA 1, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, Dal
- Universal donor: negative for DEA 1, 3, 5, 7; positive for DEA 4 (common in Greyhounds)
- Dogs do not express alloantibodies against most DEA antigens at first transfusion; subsequent exposures can sensitize
- Crossmatch required if: previous transfusion >3 days ago, history of transfusion reaction, or unknown history

#### Blood Typing — Cat
- AB system: Type A most common (70–100%); Type B (breed-specific frequencies); Type AB (rare)
- **Type B cats have strong anti-A alloantibodies → acute severe hemolytic reactions** if given Type A blood
- Type A cats: weak anti-B alloantibodies → limited lifespan of transfused type B RBCs
- Crossmatching recommended prior to ALL feline transfusions

#### Antifibrinolytic Drug Doses
| Drug | Dose | Route | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Epsilon-aminocaproic acid (EACA) | **100 mg/kg IV or PO q6h** OR **100 mg/kg IV load then 15 mg/kg/hr CRI** | IV/PO | Rapid administration → hypotension, GI signs |
| Tranexamic acid (TXA) | **10 mg/kg IV q4h** | IV | 10× more potent than EACA; vomiting in dogs esp. at ≥20 mg/kg; limited data in cats |

#### Blood Component Selection
- Fresh whole blood (FWB): ideal for acute hemorrhage; contains RBCs, plasma proteins, some platelets
- PRBCs: isolated RBC replacement; stored up to 35–42 days
- FFP: coagulation factors; 10–20 mL/kg for coagulopathy
- Plasma: 22.5 mL/kg per 0.5 g/dL albumin desired

### Ch 125 — Neurologic Crisis

> ClinIQ: ~ PROT-SEIZ (seizure management); no TBI or spinal cord protocol (candidate gaps)

#### Modified Glasgow Coma Scale (MGCS / Small Animal Coma Scale)

**Motor Activity (1–6)**
- 6 = normal gait + normal spinal reflexes
- 5 = hemiparesis/tetraparesis
- 4 = recumbent, intermittent extensor rigidity
- 3 = recumbent, constant extensor rigidity
- 2 = recumbent, constant extensor rigidity with opisthotonus
- 1 = recumbent, hypotonia, depressed/absent spinal reflexes

**Brain Stem Reflexes (1–6)**
- 6 = normal PLR + oculocephalic reflexes
- 5 = slow PLR + normal–reduced oculocephalic
- 4 = bilateral unresponsive miosis + normal–reduced oculocephalic
- 3 = pinpoint pupils + reduced–absent oculocephalic
- 2 = unilateral unresponsive mydriasis + reduced–absent oculocephalic
- 1 = bilateral unresponsive mydriasis + reduced–absent oculocephalic

**Level of Consciousness (1–6)**
- 6 = occasional alertness, responsive to environment
- 5 = depression/delirium, capable but may respond inappropriately
- 4 = semicomatose, responsive to visual stimuli
- 3 = semicomatose, responsive to auditory stimuli
- 2 = semicomatose, responsive only to repeated noxious stimuli
- 1 = comatose, unresponsive to repeated noxious stimuli

**Prognosis**: 3–8 = poor; 9–14 = guarded; 15–18 = good; ~50% survival at score 8

#### Traumatic Brain Injury (TBI) Protocol
1. Avoid jugular compression/venipuncture; elevate head ~30 degrees
2. Orotracheal intubation if non-responsive + no gag reflex
3. O₂ supplement: target PaO₂ >80 mmHg (SpO₂ >94%)
4. Ventilation: PaCO₂ 30–40 mmHg; positive pressure ventilation if needed
5. If MGCS ≤8 when hemodynamically stable → treat elevated ICP:
   - **Hypertonic saline**: 4 mL/kg 7.5% NaCl IV, OR 5 mL/kg 3% NaCl IV
   - **Mannitol**: **0.5–1 g/kg IV over 20 min**
6. Treat seizures (see below)
7. Critical monitoring: cerebral edema peaks 24–48 h post-TBI

#### Seizure / Status Epilepticus Management Protocol (Figure 125.2)

**Step 1 — Initial benzodiazepine** (IV, intranasal [IN], or per rectum [PR]):
- **Diazepam**: **0.5–2 mg/kg IV/IN/PR**
- **Midazolam**: **0.25–1 mg/kg IV/IN/PR**

**Step 2 — If seizures continue**: repeat benzodiazepine bolus 2–3 times, AND/OR:
- **Midazolam CRI**: **0.2–0.3 mg/kg/h IV CRI**
- PLUS give parenteral maintenance AED:
  - **Phenobarbital**: **2–5 mg/kg IV**
  - OR **Levetiracetam**: **20–60 mg/kg IV**

**Step 3 — If seizures stop**: initiate oral maintenance AEDs:
- **Phenobarbital**: **2–3 mg/kg PO q12h**
- OR **Levetiracetam**: **20–30 mg/kg PO q8h**
- ± **Potassium bromide (KBr) load**: **400–600 mg/kg PO divided**

**Step 4 — Refractory status epilepticus** (continue/recur):
- Continue midazolam CRI ± intermittent boluses
- OR **Propofol**: **2–6 mg/kg IV slow bolus** then **0.1–0.5 mg/kg/min IV CRI**
- OR **Ketamine**: **3–5 mg/kg IV bolus** then **0.1–0.5 mg/kg/h IV CRI**

**Long-term maintenance** (after SE or cluster):
- **Phenobarbital**: **2–5 mg/kg PO q12h**
- OR **Imepitoin**: **15 mg/kg PO q12h**
- ± **Potassium bromide**: **40 mg/kg PO q24h**
- Note: levetiracetam and zonisamide not widely recommended for initial maintenance

#### Metabolic Causes of Altered Mentation — Exclude First
- Hypoglycemia (dextrose 0.25–0.5 g/kg IV)
- Electrolyte abnormalities (Na, Ca)
- Intoxication (Ch 132/133)
- Hepatic encephalopathy

#### Spinal Cord Trauma — Prognosis
- Deep pain sensation present → fair–good prognosis; consider referral for advanced imaging ± surgery
- Deep pain sensation absent → high concern for progressive myelomalacia (10–30%), regardless of etiology

### Ch 126 — Abdominal Crisis

> ClinIQ: no dedicated abdominal crisis protocol (candidate gap); GDV content relevant to emergencies

#### Initial Approach
- Triage PE; IV access; PoC US (AFAST)
- Abdominal radiographs: right lateral recommended to differentiate GD from GDV; left lateral detects ≥2.5 mL free gas
- Free peritoneal gas on radiograph → suspect GI perforation → urgent exploratory celiotomy (unless penetrating trauma or iatrogenic)
- PoC US (EPSS sign) can detect smaller volumes of free gas than radiographs

#### Abdominocentesis / Peritoneal Fluid Analysis
- Free peritoneal fluid → sample for cytology/culture (preferably US-guided)
- **Septic effusion**: peritoneal fluid glucose >20 mg/dL below blood glucose (same cut-off as Ch 128)
- Severe coagulopathy = relative contraindication

#### Peritoneal Fluid Classification (TABLE 126.1)
(Classification by effusion type and characteristics aids in narrowing differentials — transudates, modified transudates, exudates, hemorrhagic, chylous, bilious, urine)

#### GDV Emergency Management (referenced — see Ch 261 for detail)
- Emergency decompression (gastric trocharization or orogastric tube)
- IV fluid resuscitation for shock
- ECG monitoring (ventricular arrhythmias common)
- Surgical correction urgently following stabilization

### Ch 127 — Lower Urinary Tract Crisis

> ClinIQ: no dedicated LUT unblocking protocol (candidate gap)

#### Urethral Obstruction — Overview
- Most common in male cats; also dogs
- Causes: uroliths, urethral plugs, strictures, blood clots, neoplasia
- Metabolic consequences: hyperkalemia → bradyarrhythmia; uremia; metabolic acidosis; AKI
- ~98% feline ureteroliths are calcium-containing

#### Immediate Stabilization
1. ECG: assess for hyperkalemia (bradycardia, wide QRS, tall peaked T waves)
2. IV catheter placement
3. Serum potassium + kidney values
4. Urinary catheterization (Chs 98, 99)

#### Urethral Catheterization Technique Options
- Standard red rubber or polypropylene catheter
- Hydrophilic guidewire-assisted technique: wire first, then catheter over wire
- Foley balloon catheter: cut tip to allow wire passage; smaller lumen — confirm wire compatibility
- **Antegrade catheterization** (if retrograde fails): US/fluoroscopic-guided percutaneous bladder access; guidewire directed antegrade out urethra; open-ended catheter advanced over wire; rapid decompression alternative to cystostomy

#### Ureteral Obstruction (Ch 127 also covers upper tract)
- Diagnosis: US (preferred); radiographs for most ureteroliths
- Medical management: IV fluids (restore volume, correct electrolytes); amitriptyline, glucagon, amlodipine, alpha-blockade may facilitate urolith passage
- Only 13% of cats pass stone with medical management alone; 31% show no creatinine improvement
- **Nephrostomy tubes** for temporary decompression if definitive intervention not immediately available; locking loop catheters preferred over red rubber
- **SUB (subcutaneous ureteral bypass)** or **ureteral stenting**: increasing availability; minimally invasive options
- Renal replacement therapy (hemodialysis, CRRT) for severe azotemia/refractory hyperkalemia; increases hemorrhage risk for subsequent procedures

### Ch 128 — Sepsis and Systemic Inflammatory Response Syndrome (SIRS)

> ClinIQ: no dedicated sepsis/SIRS protocol (candidate gap — high priority)

#### Definitions (BOX 128.1)
- **SIRS**: systemic inflammation (infectious or non-infectious); requires ≥2/4 criteria (dogs) or ≥3/4 criteria (cats) — see table below
- **Sepsis**: SIRS from infection; or life-threatening organ dysfunction from dysregulated host response to infection
- **Septic shock**: sepsis + hypotension requiring vasopressors to maintain MAP ≥65 mmHg OR lactate >2 mmol/L despite adequate resuscitation
- **MODS**: altered function of ≥2 organs such that homeostasis cannot be maintained without intervention

#### SIRS Criteria (TABLE 128.1)
| Parameter | Dog | Cat |
|---|---|---|
| Temperature | **>102.6°F (>37.2°C)** OR **<99°F (<39.2°C)** | **>103.5°F (>39.7°C)** OR **<100°F (<37.8°C)** |
| Heart rate (bpm) | **>140** | **>225 OR <140** |
| Respiratory rate (br/min) | **>40** | **>40** |
| WBC count | **>19,500/mcL OR <5,000/mcL OR >5% bands** | **>19,500/mcL OR <5,000/mcL OR >5% bands** |

- Diagnosis of SIRS: **≥2/4** criteria in dogs; **≥3/4** criteria in cats
- Care: do not exclude sepsis based solely on absence of SIRS criteria

#### Organ Dysfunction Criteria (TABLE 128.2) — Dogs
| System | Criterion |
|---|---|
| Renal | Creatinine ↑ ≥0.5 mg/dL (>44 micromol/L) from presurgical values without pre/post-renal azotemia |
| Cardiovascular | Hypotension requiring vasopressor therapy |
| Respiratory | Need for supplemental O₂ or mechanical ventilation; SpO₂ <95%; alveolar-arterial gradient >10 mmHg |
| Hepatic | Total bilirubin >0.5 mg/dL (>8.6 micromol/L) |
| Coagulation | PT or PTT >25% above upper reference limit AND/OR platelets ≤100,000/mcL |

#### Sepsis Treatment Protocol (Figure 128.2) — Initial Resuscitation (First 1–6 Hours)

**Hemodynamic Resuscitation:**
- IV isotonic crystalloid boluses: **dogs 15–20 mL/kg, cats 10–15 mL/kg** (NOT full shock boluses)
- Colloid boluses: **dogs 5–10 mL/kg, cats 2–5 mL/kg IV to effect**
- Full "shock" doses (dogs 90 mL/kg; cats 50 mL/kg) NOT recommended in sepsis — overhydration = worse outcomes

**Resuscitation Goals:**
- Doppler BP: >90 mmHg and <150 mmHg; MAP ≥65 mmHg
- HR: dog 70–120 bpm (breed-appropriate); cat 140–190 bpm
- SpO₂ >93% or PaO₂ >80 mmHg
- Urine output >1 mL/kg/h
- Normalize lactate, electrolytes, glucose, acid-base
- Cranial vena cava O₂ saturation ≥70% (or mixed venous O₂ saturation ≥65%)

**Vasopressor Selection (if volume fails):**
- Norepinephrine CRI (dosing per Ch 121 Table 121.3): **0.05–2 mcg/kg/min**
- Dopamine: **5–20 mcg/kg/min**
- Dobutamine (positive inotrope if myocardial dysfunction): **5–20 mcg/kg/min** — do NOT use in cats with HCM
- Vasopressin: **0.5–5 mU/kg/min**
- Epinephrine as additional vasopressor if above fail
- Consider critical illness-related corticosteroid insufficiency if refractory vasopressor dependency → hydrocortisone **0.25–1 mg/kg q6h or 0.1–0.16 mg/kg/h IV**

**Antimicrobials (within 30–60 minutes of identification):**
- Broad-spectrum, IV, bactericidal
- Empiric protocols: Ticarcillin/clavulanic acid; Ampicillin + fluoroquinolone; Clindamycin + cefotaxime; Clindamycin + fluoroquinolone; Ampicillin + amikacin; Cefoxitin; Imipenem
- Culture before antibiotics when possible

#### Long-Term Management (>6 Hours)
- Enteral nutrition preferred (feeding tube); if vomiting → parenteral nutrition
- GI protection: omeprazole, famotidine, sucralfate
- Antiemetics: ondansetron, maropitant
- Motility agents: erythromycin, metoclopramide, cisapride
- Earlier transfusion triggers in sepsis (due to tissue hypoxia sensitivity)
- Bicarbonate NOT recommended for lactic acidosis (treat perfusion instead)
- Balanced electrolyte solutions (e.g., Plasma-Lyte) preferred over 0.9% saline

### Ch 129 — Anaphylaxis

> ClinIQ: no dedicated anaphylaxis protocol (candidate gap — high priority)

#### Clinical Signs
- **Dogs**: primary shock organ = liver → hepatic venous engorgement, hypovolemia; also GI (vomiting, diarrhea), urticaria, pruritus, facial edema
- **Cats**: primary shock organ = respiratory (bronchospasm, laryngeal edema); also cardiovascular, GI signs
- Life-threatening respiratory effects in ~67% of dogs and cats with anaphylaxis
- Cardiovascular: hypotension, tachycardia, weak pulses
- Diagnostic markers: histamine + tryptase not readily available in veterinary medicine

#### Anaphylaxis Treatment Protocol (Figure 129.2)

**Step 1 — Immediate:**
1. Remove/cease exposure to inciting cause
2. O₂ by mask or flow-by; intubate + ventilate with 100% O₂ if required
3. Monitoring: ECG, SpO₂, ETCO₂, BP
4. Vascular access — if unable: **0.01 mg/kg epinephrine (EPI) IM**
5. **IV epinephrine infusion**: **0.05 mcg/kg/min** (dedicated line)
   - TIP: add 1 mg EPI to 1 L crystalloid → give 30 mL/hr per 10 kg; titrate q3–5 min; steady state may take 5–10 min
6. If hypotensive (severe reaction): **isotonic crystalloid bolus: 20 mL/kg (dog) or 15 mL/kg (cat)**, as fast as possible
7. If hypoglycemic: **dextrose 0.25–0.5 g/kg IV**

**Step 2 — If inadequate response or imminent CPA:**

*Persisting hypotension:*
- **Epinephrine**: titrate infusion up OR bolus **5–10 mcg/kg IV**
- **Atropine** (if severe bradycardia): **0.02–0.04 mg/kg IV**
- Further crystalloid boluses titrated to total: **dog 90 mL/kg; cat 60 mL/kg**
- Blood products if bleeding/coagulopathy
- Vasopressors (titrate to effect):
  - Norepinephrine: **0.1–2 mcg/kg/min**
  - Dopamine: **5–20 mcg/kg/min**
  - Vasopressin: **0.5–5 mU/kg/min**

*Bronchospasm:*
- Albuterol/salbutamol: **90/100 mcg per actuation** inhaled (spacer); repeat as needed
- OR **0.5 mL of 0.5% solution in 4 mL isotonic saline** by nebulizer
- **Terbutaline**: **0.01 mg/kg IM or IV**
- **Dexamethasone** (no immediate effect): **0.1 mg/kg IV**
- Laryngeal edema + dyspnea: intubate; if cannot intubate or oxygenate (CICO) → tracheostomy/cricothyrotomy

**Step 3 — De-escalation:**
1. Wean EPI infusion as signs resolve (watch for tachycardia, tremors, pallor, elevated BP = toxic effects)
2. Stop infusion if severe toxic signs; monitor for relapse
3. **Antihistamine**: diphenhydramine — **dogs: 1–4 mg/kg PO or IM; cats: 0.5–2 mg/kg PO or IM**
4. Monitor as in-patient **minimum 8–12 hours** for relapse

#### Evidence Notes on Adjuncts
- Antihistamines: NOT frontline; do not delay epinephrine; no quality Cochrane evidence for H1-antihistamines in anaphylaxis; promethazine has vasodilatory effects (avoid); rapid chlorpheniramine may cause hypotension
- Glucocorticoids: commonly used but evidence lacking; delayed onset limits hyperacute use; routine use not recommended (Cochrane review of 31 studies found no benefit for severity or biphasic reactions)

#### Prognosis
- Poor: hypoglycemia at presentation (5.7× more likely to die), hypocoagulability (PT/PTT increase >50% of reference = death-associated), hyperphosphatemia >12.0 mg/dL (79.6× more likely to die)
- Mortality: up to 14.9% (dogs), 13% (cats)

### Ch 130 — Hyperthermia and Hypothermia

> ClinIQ: no dedicated hyperthermia/hypothermia protocol (candidate gap)

#### HYPERTHERMIA

##### Definitions
- **Hyperthermia**: non-stressed body temperature >39.2°C (>102.5°F); must differentiate from fever
- **Heatstroke**: core temperature **>40°C (>104°F)** + neurologic impairment + multi-organ dysfunction
- **Critical apoptosis threshold**: 41.5–42.0°C (106.7–107.6°F) in liver, spleen, thymus, intestine

##### Heatstroke Diagnosis
- Based on history + PE; normothermia does NOT exclude if cooling was pre-hospital
- nRBCs ≥18/100 WBCs on blood smear: sensitivity 91%, specificity 88% for predicting death
- Hypoglycemia <60 mg/dL (3.3 mmol/L) = neuroglycopenia → altered mentation + seizures
- Coagulopathy: DIC diagnosed in 52% of heatstroke dogs; progressive PT/aPTT prolongation at 12 and 24 hrs = increased mortality
- AKI in ~33% of dogs post-heatstroke

##### Heatstroke Treatment Protocol

**Immediate:**
1. ABC triage (Ch 119); ensure airway is not the cause of hyperthermia
2. Rapid cooling: spray with **cool water** + fan — do NOT use ice water/packs (vasoconstriction traps core heat; evaporative cooling works ~2× faster than invasive methods)
3. Monitor temperature q5 minutes; **stop cooling at 39.5–39.7°C (103.0–103.5°F)** to avoid hypothermia

**IV Fluids:**
- **Room temperature** isotonic crystalloids: **dogs 20 mL/kg; cats 10 mL/kg** when hypovolemia identified; given over 20 min
- Synthetic colloids (hetastarch/tetrastarch): **5–10 mL/kg IV boluses** (caution: may potentiate coagulopathies)
- **FFP for hemorrhaging patients: 10–20 mL/kg IV**
- Vasopressor therapy if not responding to volume/transfusions

**Blood Glucose Monitoring:**
- Repeat BG monitoring first several hours
- Hypoglycemia: **50% dextrose 0.5–1 mL/kg IV** diluted 1:3 with saline as bolus; then **2.5–5% dextrose CRI** to avoid hyperglycemia

**Antibiotics (prophylactic):**
- **Ampicillin/sulbactam: 30 mg/kg IV q8h** (to prevent bacterial translocation/sepsis)
- Evidence does not confirm mortality benefit of prophylactic antibiotics in naturally occurring heatstroke

**Mannitol:**
- For cerebral edema AND/OR pigment nephropathy (forced diuresis): **0.5–1 g/kg IV over 20 min**

**Anti-Inflammatory Medications:**
- NSAIDs and corticosteroids NOT recommended (GI and renal dysfunction risk; no proven mortality benefit)

##### Heatstroke Prognosis
- Survival 50–60% even with intensive treatment
- Poor prognostic indicators: coma, hypoglycemia at presentation, refractory hypoglycemia, serum creatinine increase at 24h, hypothermia, prolonged PT/aPTT at 12 and 24h, decreased fibrinogen at 24h

---

#### HYPOTHERMIA

##### Definition and Classification
- **Hypothermia**: core body temperature **<37.2°C (<99°F)**
- Primary (accidental): cold exposure
- Secondary: underlying disease, trauma, drug administration (anesthesia = most common cause)
- Severity: mild → moderate → severe (death risk)

##### Hypothermia Clinical Effects
- Bradycardia + decreased SVR (alpha-1 receptors lose affinity for norepinephrine temperature-dependently)
- Hypothermic bradycardia **unresponsive to atropine**
- VF or asystole possible: **≤50% of dogs with core temp <23.3°C (<74°F)** have VF
- Cold diuresis → hypovolemia (increased GFR → ADH suppression)
- Hypocoagulability (may not show on standard PT/aPTT analyzers if sample warmed)

##### Hypothermia Treatment
| Severity | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Mild (hydrated) | Passive surface warming: dry fur, blankets, towels, bubble wrap |
| Moderate–severe (dehydrated) | Active surface warming: forced-air blankets, warm water bottles, heating pads |
| Severe (cardiovascular abnormalities) | Active core warming: **warmed IV fluids 40–42°C (104–107.6°F)**; warmed/humidified air (if intubated); warm water enemas; intracavitary/intravesicular warm water lavage |

- Avoid drastic temperature increases → peripheral vasodilation → rewarming shock or "After Drop"
- **Plasma transfusions NOT indicated for primary hypothermia** (case-by-case for secondary)

##### Hypothermia Prognosis
- Serum K >12 mmol/L = prognostic for non-survival (human data)

### Ch 131 — Cardiopulmonary Arrest and CPR (RECOVER)

> ClinIQ: ~ PROT-CPR

#### Diagnosis of CPA
- **Criteria**: acutely non-responsive + no attempt to breathe
- Rapid ABC assessment (5–10 seconds): palpate pulse; observe chest excursions; auscult lung sounds
- If uncertainty persists after 10 seconds → **start CPR immediately** rather than further diagnostics
- Delay in CPR initiation strongly associated with mortality

#### CPR Algorithm Overview — Immediate Priority Tasks (first 2 min)
1. Intubate (ET tube); begin positive pressure ventilation
2. Begin chest compressions
3. Establish IV or IO access
4. Initiate ECG monitoring
5. Administer first drug doses (vasopressor/atropine at 4 min intervals)

---

#### BASIC LIFE SUPPORT (BLS)

##### Chest Compressions
- **Rate**: **100–120 compressions/minute**
- **Depth**: **one-third to one-half the chest width**
- Full elastic recoil between compressions (maximize coronary/cerebral perfusion)
- Deliver in **uninterrupted 2-minute cycles**; rotate compressor after each cycle to reduce fatigue
- Optimal cardiac output with chest compressions: only ~25–40% of normal CO generated

**Compression technique by body type:**
| Patient Type | Position | Compression Location |
|---|---|---|
| Medium/large dogs — round chest (e.g., Labrador, Rottweiler) | Lateral recumbency | Highest point of lateral thoracic wall |
| Keel-chested dogs (e.g., sighthounds — deep, narrow chest) | Lateral recumbency | Directly over the heart |
| Broad-chested dogs (e.g., English Bulldogs — wider than deep) | Dorsal recumbency | Mid-sternum |
| Cats + small dogs (<10 kg) | Lateral recumbency | One-handed technique: wrap hand around sternum over heart; compress thumb against fingers (apex to base) |

##### Ventilation
- ET tube placement as soon as possible; intubation in lateral recumbency
- With inflated ET cuff: compressions and ventilations simultaneously (avoids compression pauses)
- **Ventilation rate (intubated, inflated cuff): 1 breath every 6 seconds (10/min); inspiratory time 1 second**
- Without inflated cuff (or BVM): 30:2 compression-to-ventilation ratio
- **Excessive ventilation is harmful** (decreases venous return, reduces cardiac output)

---

#### ADVANCED LIFE SUPPORT (ALS)

##### Vascular Access
- IV (peripheral or jugular) or IO (intraosseous) — IO placement faster than jugular catheterization in cadaver studies
- Drug delivery: follow IV/IO dose with flush; intratracheal (IT) route = double IV dose, diluted, via red rubber feeding tube to level of carina

##### Vasopressors (every other cycle = every 4 min)
| Drug | Standard Dose (IV/IO) | High/Extended Dose | Intratracheal |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Epinephrine (low dose — first-line)** | **0.01 mg/kg IV/IO** | **0.1 mg/kg IV/IO** (>10 min CPR; lower survival to discharge — use with caution) | **0.02 mg/kg IT** (double dose, dilute in 2 mL/10 kg sterile saline or water) |
| **Vasopressin (alternative/adjunct)** | **0.8 U/kg IV/IO** | Same schedule | **Double dose + dilute, IT** |

- Epinephrine (alpha-1): peripheral vasoconstriction → increases aortic diastolic pressure → coronary/cerebral perfusion
- Vasopressin (V1): vasoconstrictive effects; similar or higher efficacy than epinephrine; give every 4 min
- High-dose epinephrine 0.1 mg/kg: associated with **lower survival to discharge** — use only >10 min CPR and with that understanding

##### Anticholinergics (every other cycle = every 4 min)
- **Atropine**: **0.04 mg/kg IV/IO**
- Indications: non-shockable arrest rhythms; evidence of high vagal tone at time of CPA; bradycardic arrest; diseases predisposing to high vagal tone (GI, respiratory, CNS, ophthalmic diseases)
- Limited evidence of benefit or harm during CPR
- **Atropine IT dose: 0.08 mg/kg** (double, dilute, deliver to carina)

##### Reversal Agents
| Drug | Indication | Dose |
|---|---|---|
| **Naloxone** | Opioid reversal | **0.04 mg/kg IV/IO** |
| **Flumazenil** | Benzodiazepine reversal | **0.01 mg/kg IV/IO** |
| **Atipamezole** | Alpha-2 agonist reversal | **0.1 mg/kg IV/IO** |
| **Yohimbine** | Alpha-2 agonist reversal | **0.1 mg/kg IV/IO** |

##### IV Fluids During CPR
- Indicated only with **documented or suspected hypovolemia**
- **Isotonic crystalloid boluses: dogs 20–30 mL/kg; cats 10–20 mL/kg**
- **Avoid IV fluids in euvolemic or hypervolemic patients** — increases right atrial pressure without increasing aortic pressure → decreases CPP

##### ECG and Rhythm Analysis — Decision Tree
- **Consistent repeating complexes + associated pulses** → perfusing rhythm = ROSC
- **Consistent complexes + no pulses + rate >200/min** → shockable (VF/VT)
- **Consistent complexes + no pulses + rate ≤200/min** → PEA (non-shockable)
- **Flat line (asystole)** → non-shockable
- **VF** → shockable

##### Defibrillation (for shockable rhythms: VF or pulseless VT)
- Perform during or immediately after 2-minute compression cycle
- Do not interrupt compressions for rhythm check until cycle complete; defibrillate even if <2 min complete
- Immediately after defibrillation: resume compressions for another 2-min cycle (direct conversion to perfusing rhythm not likely immediately post-shock; 27% success in vet study)
- **Precordial thump**: only if defibrillator unavailable; dismal success rate; NEVER use if defibrillator available
- Monophasic and biphasic defibrillators: doses per RECOVER drug chart (weight-based volumes)

#### Open-Chest CPR (OC-CPR) Indications
- CPA during abdominal or thoracic surgery → direct cardiac massage
- Significant thoracic wall defects
- Giant breed, round-chested dogs (closed-chest CPR unlikely effective)

#### Post-ROSC Care (RECOVER)
- Goal: prevent secondary ischemia-reperfusion injury
- Maintain BP, normoglycemia, normothermia, normal ventilation
- ETCO₂ sudden increase = sign of ROSC
- Targeted temperature management: avoid hyperthermia

#### Prognosis
- Overall survival rates very low in spontaneous CPA
- Perianesthetic CPA: survival to discharge approaching **50%**
- Most important prognostic factor: underlying cause; untreatable/progressive disease = poor
- Prompt CPR initiation and high-quality BLS adherence = best modifiable factors
