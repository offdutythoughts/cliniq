### Ch 257 — Laboratory Evaluation of the GI Tract

**Author:** Sina Marsilio

#### Folate (Vitamin B9)
- Absorbed by brush-border folate deconjugase + specific folate carriers in the **proximal** small intestine only (no appreciable absorption in distal SI or colon).
- **Decreased** serum folate → diffuse proximal SI mucosal infiltration / malabsorption.
- **Increased** serum folate → intestinal dysbiosis (bacterial production).
- Diagnostic utility is lower than that of cobalamin for clinical decision-making (Ch 257).

#### Cobalamin (Vitamin B12)
- Absorbed in the **distal** SI (ileum) via cubam receptor (cubilin + amnionless).
- Intrinsic factor (IF) produced predominantly by **exocrine pancreas** in dogs and cats (minority from gastric parietal cells).
- **EPI** is an important differential for hypocobalaminemia (IF deficiency → cobalamin malabsorption).
- Hypocobalaminemia → cobalamin-dependent metabolic reactions impaired intracellularly; serum level may not fully reflect cellular deficiency → methylmalonic acid (MMA) and homocysteine (HCY) are better markers of cellular deficiency (Ch 257).
- **Reference interval**: serum cobalamin <200 ng/mL = negative prognostic factor in canine CE (Ch 262).
- Low or low-normal serum B12 **or** elevated MMA → supplementation indicated (Ch 262).
- Hereditary cobalamin deficiency documented in: Australian Shepherd, Beagle, Border Collie, Komondor, Shar-Pei, Yorkshire Terrier, and others (Ch 262).

#### Fecal Alpha-1 Proteinase Inhibitor (Alpha-1 PI)
- Synthesized in liver; MW ~60,000 Da (similar to albumin); **not** hydrolyzed by GI proteases.
- Lost at similar rate to albumin in PLE → used as biomarker of GI protein loss.
- **Caveat 1**: can be falsely normal in dogs with severe hypoalbuminemia + GI protein loss.
- **Caveat 2**: falsely abnormal with digitally retrieved (finger-collected) fecal samples.
- Only available at GI Lab, Texas A&M University (Ch 257).

#### Serum C-Reactive Protein (CRP)
- Increases in dogs with idiopathic IBD; correlates with CIBDAI score.
- Greatest utility: **monitoring treatment response** in dogs with IBD.
- Decreases with effective dietary or medical therapy (Ch 257).

#### Dysbiosis Index (DI)
- Mathematical algorithm based on 7 bacterial taxa + total bacteria in feces.
- **DI >2** = consistent with dysbiosis (74% sensitivity, 95% specificity for differentiating dogs with CE from healthy dogs) (Ch 257).

#### PARR (Polymerase Chain Reaction for Antigen Receptor Rearrangement)
- Assesses T- or B-cell receptor clonality.
- Used to differentiate feline IBD from low-grade intestinal T-cell lymphoma (LGITL / SCL).
- **Caveat**: clonality not specific for LGITL; found with chronic antigenic stimulation and normal aging. Results alone insufficient to reclassify IBD→SCL (Ch 257).

**> ClinIQ: ✓ EPI** — TLI cutoff not stated in Ch 257; referenced to Ch 279.
**> ClinIQ: ✓ IBD** — CRP monitoring mentioned; DI utility.
**> ClinIQ: ✗ MISSING** — Cobalamin deficiency (as its own entity); good candidate for ClinIQ entry.

### Ch 258 — Oral Cavity and Salivary Gland Disorders

**Authors:** Alexander M. Reiter, Maria M. Soltero-Rivera

#### Perioperative Considerations (Dentistry/Oral Surgery)
- Prolonged wide mouth opening → maxillary artery blood flow reduction → temporary or permanent blindness and/or neurological deficits in **cats** (use rigid plastic cylinders 20–30 mm between canines).
- Oral edema prevention: single injection **dexamethasone 0.1–0.2 mg/kg IV** at end of procedure.
- Diffuse nasal mucosa bleeding (post-maxillectomy/palate surgery): **0.05–0.1 mL/kg (cats) or 0.1–0.2 mL/kg (dogs)** of a mixture of 0.25 mL 1% phenylephrine and 50 mL 2% lidocaine.

#### Stomatitis (Feline)
- Caudal oral cavity inflammation extending to gingiva, alveolar and buccal mucosa.
- FCV, FHV-1 implicated; fungi may also play a role.
- **Treatment doses:**
  - Methylprednisolone acetate (Depo-Medrol): **4 mg/kg SC or IM** (periodic injections)
  - Prednisolone ointment: **1 mg/kg per 0.1 mL q12–24h** applied to pinna
  - Cyclosporine (Neoral): **start 2.5 mg/kg PO q12h for 6 weeks** before judging effectiveness; whole-blood cyclosporine >300 ng/mL associated with significant improvement of oral inflammation.
  - Bovine lactoferrin: **250 mg PO q24h**
  - Doxycycline: **1 mg/kg PO q24h**
  - Interferon omega: 5 MU diluted, injected submucosally into all inflamed areas; remainder added to 100 mL NaCl, frozen in 10 mL aliquots; client gives 1 mL PO q24h for 100 days.
  - Autologous adipose-derived MSCs: complete resolution or reduction reported in most cats with severe refractory stomatitis.

#### Eosinophilic Granuloma Complex (dogs/cats)
- **Treatment:** prednisone or prednisolone **1–2 mg/kg PO q12h** initially, then taper to lowest effective dosage; methylprednisolone **4 mg/kg SC or IM** periodically; amoxicillin-clavulanate 14 mg/kg PO q12h.

#### Masticatory Muscle Myositis (MMM)
- Autoimmune; temporal, masseter, medial/lateral pterygoid muscles affected.
- **Diagnosis:** type 2M fiber antibody titer <1:100 negative; 1:100 borderline; >1:100 positive.
- **Treatment:**
  - Acute: dexamethasone **0.2–0.4 mg/kg IV** once after biopsy (immediate inflammation reduction)
  - Discharge: prednisone **1–2 mg/kg PO q12h** → rapid improvement 1–2 days → decrease to 1 mg/kg PO q24h for 3–4 weeks → slowly taper to lowest alternate-day dose over **8–12 months**.
  - Refractory/unresponsive/unacceptable side-effects: azathioprine **1–2 mg/kg PO q24h**.
  - Recheck: 2 weeks, then 1, 2, 6, 9, 12 months; then q6–12 months.
  - Periodic antibody titer tests prior to reducing prednisone (especially at very low doses, e.g., 0.1–0.2 mg/kg PO q48h).

#### Salivary Gland Diseases

**Sialocele:** Surgical treatment required (see surgical textbooks).

**Sialadenosis / Necrotizing Sialometaplasia:**
- Phenobarbital **1–2 mg/kg PO q12h** resulted in dramatic improvement (neurogenic pathogenesis support).

**Autoimmune Oral Conditions (Pemphigus/Pemphigoid):**
- Prednisone or prednisolone **1–2 mg/kg PO q12h** initially, then taper to lowest effective dosage; or methylprednisolone **4 mg/kg SC or IM** periodically.

**Uremia (oral manifestations):**
- Sucralfate **0.5–1 g PO q4–6h**; buprenorphine **0.01 mg/kg q8–12h, IV** for pain management.

**> ClinIQ: ✓ DIS-DENT-ORONASAL** — Oronasal fistula covered.
**> ClinIQ: ✗ MISSING** — Salivary Mucocele/Sialocele (candidate), MMM (candidate — falls under oral/neuromuscular). Feline stomatitis is a candidate for its own ClinIQ entry.

### Ch 259 — Pharyngeal and Esophageal Diseases

**Author:** Stanley Leon Marks

#### Myasthenia Gravis (MG) — Related to Megaesophagus
- AChR antibody titer "gray zone": **0.3–0.6 nmol/L** in dogs — retest in 4–8 weeks if onset is acute or titer is borderline.
- MG accounts for **25–30%** of acquired secondary megaesophagus (ASM) in dogs.
- Focal MG (esophagus only) in **40%** of MG-megaesophagus dogs.
- **Treatment:** pyridostigmine **1–3 mg/kg PO q8–12h**; predniso(lo)ne **1–2 mg/kg PO, SC q12h** for myasthenic polymyopathy (from Table 259.1).
- Prognosis: approximately **50%** of MG megaesophagus dogs respond to supportive therapy.

#### Esophagitis
- Most common cause of high-grade esophagitis and stricture: GER during general anesthesia (**46–65%** of stricture cases).
- GER during anesthesia incidence: **16–55%** in dogs; causes include atropine, morphine, acepromazine, thiopental, xylazine, isoflurane.
- Esophageal mucosal injury especially when pH <4.0 (pepsin activation range: pH 1.5–3.5).
- **Treatment doses:**
  - PPIs (omeprazole, pantoprazole, esomeprazole): **1–1.5 mg/kg PO or IV q12h** for 6–8 weeks (moderate-severe); **1–2 weeks** mild cases.
  - Sucralfate suspension: **0.5–1 g PO q8h** (as slurry).
  - Cisapride (prokinetic): **0.5–1 mg/kg PO q8–12h**.
  - Metoclopramide: **0.2–0.4 mg/kg SC or IV bolus q8h**, or **1–2 mg/kg/24h IV CRI**.
- Note: sucralfate + PPI should be spaced to avoid decreased efficacy.
- Note: cisapride is superior prokinetic to metoclopramide; more effective at reducing GER in dogs.

#### Esophageal Stricture
- Clinical signs appear ~7–8 days post-anesthesia.
- **Treatment:** balloon dilation (repeated 5–7 days later); intraluminal stenting for failures.
- Transendoscopic triamcinolone: **~2.5 mg per quadrant** (4-quadrant injection) before balloon dilation — for first 2–3 procedures.
- Continue medical therapy post-dilation: same medications as for esophagitis.

**> ClinIQ: ✗ MISSING** — Esophagitis (candidate). Esophageal Stricture (candidate). Both common clinical entities without dedicated ClinIQ entries.

#### Vascular Ring Anomaly / PRAA
- PRAA = most common vascular ring anomaly in dogs and cats.
- Hereditary basis in German Shepherd Dogs and Irish Setters.
- Signs at weaning (~4–6 weeks): regurgitation of solid foods, failure to thrive.
- Radiograph: **leftward deviation of trachea** near cranial border of heart on DV/VD = reliable sign of PRAA in dogs.
- **Treatment:** surgical ligation and transection of ligamentum arteriosum (left intercostal thoracotomy or thoracoscopically); balloon dilation at stricture site; gastrostomy tube pre-surgery if severely malnourished; aspiration pneumonia treated with broad-spectrum antibiotics.
- Prognosis: significant improvement in >90%; esophageal hypomotility/regurgitation may persist, especially if surgery delayed.

**> ClinIQ: ✗ MISSING** — Vascular Ring Anomaly/PRAA (candidate for new entry).

#### Esophageal Foreign Body (OESFB)
- Most common in dogs: bones, fishhooks, needles, sticks.
- Lodge at: thoracic inlet, heart base, or diaphragmatic hiatus.
- **Treatment post-removal:** fast 24–48 hours; gastrostomy tube if severe esophagitis/necrosis; sucralfate + PPIs for **2–4 weeks**.

**> ClinIQ: ✓ OESFB** — Esophageal FB covered.

#### Megaesophagus
- **Congenital:** familial predisposition: Irish Setter, Great Dane, GSD, Labrador, Chinese Shar-Pei, Newfoundland, Miniature Schnauzer, Fox Terrier; Siamese cats.
- **Acquired:** idiopathic (most common), or secondary (ASM) to MG (25–30%), hypoadrenocorticism, SLE, polymyopathies, polyneuropathies, dysautonomia, lead, GDV, esophagitis, etc.
- Key investigation: AChR antibody titer even without generalized weakness; resting cortisol or ACTH stim test to rule out hypoadrenocorticism.
- Hypothyroidism: **association with megaesophagus not proven** — low T4 more consistent with euthyroid sick syndrome.
- **Prokinetics CONTRAINDICATED** in megaesophagus (cisapride, metoclopramide increase LES tone → exacerbate "achalasia-like" syndrome; no effect on esophageal striated muscle).
- **Exception:** cisapride may be useful in **cats** for distal esophageal motility disturbances (smooth muscle).
- Sildenafil **1 mg/kg PO q12h** (author recommends q8h due to short half-life) — associated with reduction in regurgitation episodes, increased weight gain, decreased esophageal diameter in puppies with congenital megaesophagus.
- Standard supportive: small frequent meals from **elevated/upright position** (Bailey chair); vary food consistency (liquid, meatballs, kibble); gastrostomy tube for severe/intractable aspiration pneumonia; broad-spectrum antibiotics for aspiration pneumonia.

**> ClinIQ: ✓ DIS-OES-MEGA** — Megaoesophagus covered. Enrichments: sildenafil dose, AChR titer gray-zone, prokinetics contraindicated, hypothyroidism-megaesophagus association caveat.

#### Hiatal Hernia
- Type I (sliding) most common; congenital in brachycephalic breeds (Chinese Shar-Pei, Chow Chow, English Bulldog, French Bulldog, Pug, Boston Terrier) — signs within first 12 months.
- Endoscopy: apparent separation between squamocolumnar junction and diaphragmatic impression **>2 cm** = sliding hiatal hernia on J-maneuver.
- **Treatment:** PPIs + sucralfate (spaced apart); cisapride or metoclopramide; fat-restricted diet.
- Surgical: diaphragmatic crural apposition + esophagopexy + left fundic tube gastropexy.

**> ClinIQ: ✓ HH** — Hiatal hernia covered. Enrichment: Type I–IV classification, endoscopic 2 cm threshold.

#### GER (Gastroesophageal Reflux)
- Most common causes: hiatal hernia, anesthesia-induced LES pressure reduction, chronic vomiting, gastric atony/pyloric outflow obstruction.
- **Treatment:** PPIs + sucralfate (spaced) + cisapride/metoclopramide; fat-restricted diet; fundoplication in refractory cases.

#### Esophageal Neoplasia
- Rare (<0.5% of all cancers in dogs/cats).
- Fibrosarcoma and osteosarcoma most common in dogs (from Spirocerca lupi granulomas).
- SCC most common primary esophageal tumor in cats.
- **Spirocerca lupi granuloma treatment:** doramectin **200 mcg/kg SC** at 14-day intervals × 3 treatments; or oral doramectin solution **500 mcg/kg PO q24h** for 6 weeks (repeat 6 weeks if granuloma persists on esophagoscopy).

### Ch 261 — Gastric Diseases

**Author:** M. Katherine Tolbert

#### Gastric pH
- Mean mucosal gastric pH dogs and cats: generally **1.5–3.0** (varies widely; single measurement of gastric fluid to assess acid suppression is not meaningful).

#### Antiemetics (Table 261.2)
| Drug | Mechanism | Dose |
|---|---|---|
| Maropitant | NK1 receptor antagonist | **1 mg/kg SC or IV q24h** (dog, cat); **2 mg/kg PO q24h** dog (extralabel cat) |
| Ondansetron | 5-HT3 antagonist | **0.5 mg/kg PO* or IV q8–12h** (*poor oral bioavailability) |
| Dolasetron | 5-HT3 antagonist | **0.6–1 mg/kg PO* or IV q24h** |
| Metoclopramide | D2 antagonist | **0.2–0.5 mg/kg PO, IV, SQ q6–8h**; or **1 mg/kg IV CRI** (poor antiemetic in cats) |
| Chlorpromazine | D2 antagonist | **0.1–0.5 mg/kg SC q8h** (poor antiemetic in cats) |

#### Gastroprotectant Drugs (Box 261.1)
- **Famotidine (H2RA):** 1 mg/kg PO, SC, IV q12h; for severe ulceration: 1 mg/kg IV bolus then 8 mg/kg/day CRI. (*Note: repeated famotidine causes diminished efficacy; combination famotidine + PPI not recommended as no additional pH effect.*)
- **PPIs (omeprazole, pantoprazole, esomeprazole):** 1 mg/kg PO, IV, SC q12h. (*Lansoprazole may not be effective orally in cats.*)
- **Sucralfate slurry:** 0.25–1 g PO q6–8h (crushed tab dissolved in water; 0.25 g cats → 1 g large dogs).
- **Barium:** 1–2 mL/kg PO q8–12h, or per rectum (colorectal bleeding).
- **Misoprostol:** 3 mcg/kg PO q8–12h (dogs only; for NSAID-induced ulceration; prostaglandin E1 analog).

#### NSAID-Induced Ulceration
- Most common cause of severe gastric ulceration in dogs (1/3 of dogs needing packed RBC transfusions in one study).
- PPI therapy q12h for **3–4 weeks** in NSAID-induced ulceration; once-daily oral esomeprazole may be effective in some dogs (based on healthy Beagle study).
- Sucralfate: **0.25 g (cats) to 1 g (large dogs)** as slurry q6–8h.
- Low-fiber, low-fat, high-moisture diet; multiple small meals.

**> ClinIQ: ✓ ULC** — Gastric Ulceration covered. Enrichments: famotidine tachyphylaxis caveat, combination H2RA+PPI not recommended, misoprostol dose.

#### Blood:Urea Nitrogen:Creatinine Ratio
- **>27:1** in well-hydrated dog not on high-protein diet → increased suspicion for upper GI bleeding (low sensitivity; warrants further evaluation) (Ch 261).

#### Helicobacter-Associated Gastritis
- GHLO prevalence in healthy pets: 50–100%; vomiting pets: 41–100%.
- Pathogenic role still debated (loss of tolerance likely).
- **Diagnosis:** rapid urease test (CLO test) on biopsy; histopathology + Warthin-Starry/Giemsa/toluidine blue stain; PCR for definitive species ID.
- **Treatment — Triple Therapy:** amoxicillin **20 mg/kg PO q8–12h** + metronidazole **20 mg/kg PO q8–12h** + omeprazole **1 mg/kg PO q12–24h** — reasonable in animals failing other therapies with evidence of gastric inflammation + GHLOs.

**> ClinIQ: ✓ HELICO** — Helicobacter Gastritis covered. Enrichment: triple therapy doses.

#### Chronic Gastritis — Immunomodulatory Treatment
- Prednisolone/prednisone: **1–2 mg/kg PO q12h** (large dogs: not more than 30 mg PO q12h); taper in decremental steps of 25–30% over several weeks/months.
- Budesonide: **3 mg/m² q24h** (requires compounding; high first-pass metabolism; useful in diabetics or where systemic effects are a concern).
- Cyclosporine and chlorambucil: alternatives for steroid-refractory disease.

**> ClinIQ: ✓ CHRONGAST** — Chronic Gastritis covered. Enrichment: budesonide dose, prednisone cap at 30 mg q12h.

#### Stomach Worms
- Physaloptera spp. (dogs): centrifugal fecal flotation often false-negative; treat empirically with pyrantel pamoate **15 mg/kg PO** repeated in 2–3 weeks.
- Ollulanus tricuspis (cats): up to 20% prevalence; fecal flotation rarely diagnostic; treat empirically with fenbendazole **50 mg/kg PO q24h for 3 days**.

#### Gastric Neoplasia
**Carcinoma (dogs):**
- Gastric carcinomas: poor prognosis (<3 months historically); with surgical resection + adjuvant chemo: median survival ~6 months.
- Metastatic rate 50–90% at diagnosis; sites: gastric LN, esophagus, duodenum, spleen, liver, omentum, adrenals, lungs.
- Negative prognostic indicators for chronic gastritis progression to carcinoma: older age, thin BCS, markedly increased serum CRP, low serum folate in susceptible breed.

**Gastric Lymphoma (cats):**
- Median clinical remission: 108 days; overall survival: 171 days (when exclusively gastric).
- Multi-agent (CHOP, L-CHOP) or single-agent oral (chlorambucil + prednisolone).

**> ClinIQ: ✓ LYMP** — Gastric Lymphoma covered. ✓ CHRONGAST.

#### Gastric Dysmotility / Delayed Gastric Emptying
- Mean half gastric emptying time: by 8 hours most of a meal should have passed to duodenum.
- Distension or vomiting **>8–12 hours** post-meal → consider motility disorder.

**Prokinetics:**
| Drug | Mechanism | Dose |
|---|---|---|
| Cisapride | 5-HT4 agonist | 0.1–0.5 mg/kg PO q8–12h; 2.5 mg/cat (not mg/kg) — preferred for delayed gastric/esophageal emptying |
| Metoclopramide | D2 antagonist + weak 5-HT4 | 0.2–0.5 mg/kg SQ or IV bolus or 1–2 mg/kg/day CRI; 0.4 mg/kg SQ q8h (cats) |
| Erythromycin | Motilin receptor agonist | 0.5–1 mg/kg PO or IV (low dose) — higher doses cause retrograde peristalsis |

- Lubiprostone (48 mcg oral) accelerated post-prandial gastric emptying and SI-colonic transit in healthy dogs (novel data).

**> ClinIQ: ✓ PYL** — Pyloric Stenosis covered under gastric outflow section. ✗ MISSING — Gastric Outflow Obstruction (functional/non-neoplastic) as dedicated entry is lacking.

#### Gastric Dilation-Volvulus (GDV)
- Overall mortality: **36%** (including dogs euthanized or dying pre-surgery); surgical mortality: 11.3–18.3%.
- **Negative prognostic indicators:** delay to intervention, persistent hyperlactatemia (>**6 mg/dL** = >0.67 mmol/L), acute kidney injury, gastric wall necrosis, elevated serum myoglobin, elevated serum CRP.
- Dogs undergoing surgery **>3 hours post-admission** → poorer prognosis.
- **Decompression sedation:** pure mu opioid (methadone 0.2–0.5 mg/kg IV; hydromorphone 0.05–0.2 mg/kg; or fentanyl 3.5 mcg/kg) + midazolam 0.1–0.5 mg/kg IV.
- **Gastropexy:** approximately 5–11% of dogs have recurrent gastric dilatation post-gastropexy.

**> ClinIQ: ✓ GDV** — GDV covered. Enrichments: lactate >6 mg/dL threshold (noted as >6 mg/dL in text, consistent with mmol-level), surgery >3h prognosis, sedation protocol.

### Ch 262 — Small Intestinal Diseases

**Authors:** (Jablonski, Cridge, and team — based on client sheet)

#### Cobalamin Supplementation Protocol (Ch 262, critical clinical data)

**Oral cobalamin supplementation** (dogs with hypocobalaminemia):
- <8 kg: **250 mcg PO q24h** for 12 weeks
- 8–16 kg: **500 mcg PO q24h** for 12 weeks
- >16 kg: **1000 mcg PO q24h** for 12 weeks

**Parenteral (SC) cobalamin supplementation** (dogs with hypocobalaminemia):
- <4.5 kg: **250 mcg SC once weekly** for 6 weeks
- 4.5–9 kg: **400 mcg SC once weekly** for 6 weeks
- 9–18 kg: **600 mcg SC once weekly** for 6 weeks
- 18–27 kg: **800 mcg SC once weekly** for 6 weeks
- 27–36 kg: **1000 mcg SC once weekly** for 6 weeks
- 36–45 kg: **1200 mcg SC once weekly** for 6 weeks
- >45 kg: **1500 mcg SC once weekly** for 6 weeks

**Cats:** supplementation recommended even if serum cobalamin not measured (hypocobalaminemia in 78% of cats with GI lymphoma in one study); oral vs. parenteral route equivalency has been demonstrated in cats and dogs with CIE or EPI (Ch 262).
**Note:** following initiation, supplementation intervals are highly variable clinically; dog with IGS (inherited cobalamin deficiency) maintained on supplementation every 4–5 months — more frequent supplementation recommended.

**> ClinIQ: ✓ IBD, EPI, PLE, ARD, DRD** — cobalamin protocol applicable to all. ✗ MISSING — Cobalamin Deficiency as standalone disease entry.

#### TLI (Trypsin-Like Immunoreactivity)
- Fasted serum canine TLI: normal excludes EPI as cause of hypoalbuminemia (exact cutoff referenced to Ch 279 = <2.5 µg/L for EPI diagnosis in dogs; feline TLI cutoff also in Ch 279).
- TLI measurement recommended in chronic enteropathy workup when EPI is a differential.

**> ClinIQ: ✓ EPI** — TLI referenced to Ch 279; cutoff <2.5 µg/L consistent with standard reference.

#### Canine Chronic Enteropathy (CE) Classification
CE is classified as:
- **FRE** (food-responsive enteropathy) — also called Dietary-Responsive Disease (DRD)
- **ARE** (antibiotic-responsive enteropathy) / **Dysbiosis**
- **SRE/IRE** (steroid-responsive enteropathy / immunosuppressive-responsive enteropathy) = IBD sensu stricto

#### CCECAI and CIBDAI Scoring Systems (Table 262.4)
Both indices assess: attitude/activity, appetite, vomiting frequency, stool consistency, stool frequency, and weight loss (0–3 points each).

**CCECAI has 3 additional variables not in CIBDAI:**
1. **Serum albumin:** >2 g/dL (0), 1.5–1.99 g/dL (1), 1.2–1.49 g/dL (2), <1.2 g/dL (3)
2. **Ascites/peripheral edema:** none (0), mild (1), moderate (2), severe (3)
3. **Pruritus:** none (0), occasional (1), regular but stops during sleep (2), regularly wakes due to itching (3)

**Interpretation (both CCECAI and CIBDAI):**
- 0–3: Clinically insignificant
- 4–5: Mild disease
- 6–8: Moderate disease
- **CIBDAI ≥9: Severe disease**
- **CCECAI 9–11: Severe disease; ≥12: Very severe disease**

**Negative prognostic factors in canine CE:**
- CCECAI >8 or abnormal CIBDAI
- BUN >20 mg/dL (>7 mmol/L)
- High endoscopic score in duodenum
- Hypocobalaminemia (<200 ng/mL)
- Requirement for immunosuppressive therapy
- Hypovitaminosis D (in PLE)

**> ClinIQ: ✓ IBD** — Enrichment: full CCECAI + CIBDAI scoring tables, all negative prognostic factors.

#### Feline Chronic Enteropathy Activity Index (FCEAI) (Table 262.5)
Variables: attitude/activity, appetite, vomiting frequency, diarrhea evaluation, weight loss, endoscopic lesions, total protein, serum ALT, ALP, and serum **phosphorus** concentration.
- Used for assessing clinical severity and monitoring treatment response in cats with IRE/IBD and FRE.

#### Dietary Trial Duration
- Food-responsive disease: mild to moderate improvement within **2-week** strict dietary trial.
- Multiple different diet trials may be required before declaring failure.
- Immunosuppressive therapy considered only if dietary management fails and biopsy indicates.

#### WSAVA GI Standardization Group — Histopathology
- Normal gastric body: 0–2 eosinophils/10,000 mcm²; normal gastric antrum: 0–6 eosinophils/10,000 mcm².
- Guidelines for histopathologic evaluation of GI inflammation published by WSAVA IG Standardization Group (Washabau et al., J Vet Intern Med. 2010;24:10–26).
- Grading categories: normal, mild, moderate, marked, severe; infiltrate type; morphologic changes; lesion severity.

#### Treatment Principles for CE (Box 262.12 — Treatment Pyramid)
**Dietary:** limited antigen (novel protein or hydrolyzed); low-fat (ultra-low fat <20 g fat/1000 kcal for lymphangiectasia); highly digestible; fiber modification.
**Microbiome:** prebiotics/probiotics/synbiotics; FMT.
**Anti-inflammatory/Immunosuppressive (Chs 144, 169):**
- First line: prednisone (dogs) / prednisolone (dogs/cats); budesonide (dogs); modified cyclosporine (dogs/cats).
- Second line: mycophenolate mofetil (dogs — use with caution due to GI side-effects); azathioprine (dogs only); chlorambucil (dogs/cats); sulfasalazine (dogs — mainly for colitis).
- Highly refractory: octreotide (intestinal lymphangiectasia); heparan sulfate.
- Miscellaneous: cobalamin supplementation (if low or low-normal serum B12 or high MMA); anthelmintic; motility modification; enteral nutrition.

#### Prednisolone Dosing for CE/PLE (dogs)
- **Anti-inflammatory dosage (IL/lymphangiectasia):** 0.5–1 mg/kg PO q24h, or divided q12h; higher (immunosuppressive) dosages discouraged for IL.
- **Immunosuppressive for CIE/PLE:** not to exceed **2 mg/kg/day or 60 mg/dog** of predniso(lo)ne.
- Cyclosporine for steroid-refractory cases: **5 mg/kg PO q24h**.
- Chlorambucil for steroid-refractory PLE: **4–6 mg/m² q24h PO for 7–14 days initially**, then consider dosage reduction.

**> ClinIQ: ✓ PLE** — Enrichments: anti-inflammatory vs. immunosuppressive dose distinction, clopidogrel, calcitriol protocol, chlorambucil dose.

#### Protein-Losing Enteropathy (PLE)
- Most common causes: CIE (68%) and IL (58%) among 469 dogs with PLE.
- **Diet:** low-fat (15–22.5% ME) or ultra-low fat (10–15% ME; 1.1–1.5 g/100 kcal) for IL.
- **Thromboprophylaxis:** clopidogrel **2–3 mg/kg PO q24h** (if no contraindications).
- **Ionized hypocalcemia:** calcitriol **20–30 nanograms/kg PO q24h for 3–4 days**, then **5–15 nanograms/kg PO q24h**; and/or calcium carbonate **25–50 mg/kg PO q24h** — *caveat: calcitriol may result in hypercalcemia and hyperphosphatemia*.
- Colloids for oncotic support; effusions removed only if causing discomfort/respiratory difficulty; **diuretics discouraged**.

**PLE Etiologies:**
- Mucosal injury/permeability: CIE, infections (fungal, parasitic, viral, bacterial), neoplasia, ulceration, chronic obstruction, intestinal crypt disease, hypoadrenocorticism.
- Lymphatic disease: primary IL, secondary IL (CIE, intestinal neoplasia, right heart failure, cardiac tamponade, portal hypertension), focal lipogranulomatous lymphangitis.

**> ClinIQ: ✓ PLE** — Covered. Enrichments: clopidogrel dose, calcitriol protocol, fat % targets.

#### Feline Small Cell Lymphoma (SCL) — Treatment
- **Chlorambucil:** 2 mg/cat PO q2–3 days; OR pulse-dose protocol **20 mg/m² PO every 2 weeks**.
- Combined with **prednisolone**.
- Overall response rates: **76–96%**; median duration of clinical remission: **428–897 days**.
- Rescue with cyclophosphamide if recurrence on chlorambucil: successful in 7/9 cats in one study.
- MST for cats with SCL: 416 days; 3-year survival rate: 15% vs. 71% for IRE/IBD.

**> ClinIQ: ✓ LYMP** (intestinal lymphoma section) — Enrichment: chlorambucil dosing protocols, remission rates, MST. Full feline SCL data should be cross-linked to ClinIQ IBD/SCL entries.

#### AHDS (Acute Hemorrhagic Diarrhea Syndrome)
- Previously called HGE; sudden onset severe hemorrhagic diarrhea ± vomiting.
- C. perfringens overgrowth suspected (positive fecal PCR for netE/netF type A strains = suggestive, not confirmatory).
- **Treatment:** IV fluids at rapid rates until PCV in mid-normal range; antibiotics NOT required in uncomplicated AHDS (no sepsis); improvement typically within 24–48 hours.
- Antibiotic restriction: only for systemic complications, evidence of systemic inflammation, neutropenic/immunosuppressed, portosystemic shunt/liver dysfunction.
- Buprenorphine: preferred analgesic in AHDS (less gastric motility suppression).
- FMT being investigated as adjunct treatment.

**> ClinIQ: ✓ AHDS** — Covered. Enrichments: antibiotics not required in uncomplicated AHDS, PCV endpoint, buprenorphine preference.

#### Intussusception
- Characteristic ultrasound: concentric ring / "bull's-eye" appearance; evidence of blood flow within = may be reducible by gentle pressure. Surgical resection + end-to-end anastomosis often required.
- Cobalamin supplementation recommended post-intussusception (see protocol above).

**> ClinIQ: ✓ DIS-GI-INTUSS** — Covered.

#### Intestinal Lymphangiectasia (IL)
- **Diagnosis:** white-spotted mucosa on endoscopy; dilated lymphatics on histopathology.
- Dietary treatment: ultra-low fat (<20 g fat/1000 kcal) essential; anti-inflammatory prednisolone 0.5–1 mg/kg PO q24h (higher doses discouraged).
- Ultra-low fat diet was effective in dogs with IL refractory to prednisolone.

**> ClinIQ: ✓ PLE** — IL included. Enrichment: ultra-low fat diet precise fat % thresholds.

#### Canine Small Cell Lymphoma (SCL)
- Median survival for 14 dogs with T-cell SCL: **628 days** (mostly treated with chlorambucil, cyclophosphamide, and/or lomustine).

#### Dysbiosis / ARE (Antibiotic-Responsive Enteropathy)
- Tylosin-responsive enteropathy → clinical signs similar to dogs with IBD.
- Secondary dysbiosis causes: rule out EPI, primary SI disease before diagnosing primary SI dysbiosis.
- Metronidazole and tylosin both associated with **prolonged dysbiosis** despite amelioration of clinical signs — caution with indiscriminate antibiotic use.

**> ClinIQ: ✓ ARD** — Antibiotic-responsive/dysbiosis covered. Enrichment: prolonged dysbiosis with both metronidazole and tylosin.

### Ch 263 — Diseases of the Large Intestine

**Authors:** Stefan Unterer, Melanie Werner

#### Diagnostic Lab Thresholds — Colon
- Serum T4 measurement: recommended in cats >7 years with LI signs (Ch 263, referenced diagnostic approach).
- Pseudo-hypoadrenocorticism with Trichuris: hyponatremia + hyperkalemia → normal ACTH stimulation test.

#### Acute Colitis
- Self-limiting; supportive care (highly digestible diet, ± fiber) is first-line.
- Antibiotics for acute colitis: not recommended except for patients with systemic inflammation signs (used as last choice); amoxicillin, tylosin, or metronidazole may be considered.
- Corticosteroids: avoid unless infectious colitis has been ruled out.

**> ClinIQ: ✓ COLITIS** — Chronic Colitis covered. Enrichments: complete drug table with doses.

#### Granulomatous Colitis (GC) / Histiocytic Ulcerative Colitis
- Boxer dogs, French Bulldogs predisposed; caused by adherent/invasive E. coli.
- **Diagnosis:** large, foamy PAS-positive macrophages on colonic biopsy → confirm E. coli by FISH or IHC.
- **Treatment:** culture colonic mucosa + antimicrobial sensitivity testing → targeted antibiotics. Empirical broad-spectrum: amoxicillin-clavulanic acid or ampicillin-sulbactam **10–25 mg/kg IV q8h** ± marbofloxacin **2–4 mg/kg IV q24h**.
- Positive long-term outcomes with targeted antimicrobials after biopsy + FISH/IHC confirmation.

**> ClinIQ: ✓ GRANCOL** — Granulomatous Colitis covered. Enrichment: empirical antibiotic doses.

#### Trichuris vulpis (Whipworm)
- Eggs shed intermittently → routine fecal flotation may be false-negative; repeat if high suspicion.
- Pseudo-hypoadrenocorticism: hyponatremia + hyperkalemia with normal ACTH stimulation test → empirical anthelmintic before baseline cortisol in dogs with these electrolyte changes.

**> ClinIQ: ✓ WHIP** — Whipworm covered. Enrichment: pseudo-hypoadrenocorticism electrolyte pattern.

#### Tritrichomonas foetus
- Cats only; large bowel diarrhea; young purebred cats in multi-cat households at risk.
- **Diagnosis:** direct fecal smear, cage-side culture pouch, or **real-time PCR** (only PCR has acceptable sensitivity; submit feces fresh or in 70% isopropyl alcohol for DNA preservation).
- **Treatment:** ronidazole **30 mg/kg PO q24h for 14 days**.
  - If diarrhea persists >2 weeks despite therapy: repeat PCR + consider ronidazole **30 mg/kg PO q12h**.
  - Neurotoxicosis: reversible in most cases but common enough that treatment should only be initiated with confirmed diagnosis.
  - Spontaneous resolution without treatment: 5 months to 2 years; time to treatment response longer in multi-cat households and cats with prior antibiotics.

**> ClinIQ: ✓ TRICHO** — Tritrichomonas covered. Enrichment: twice-daily dose protocol for refractory cases, neurotoxicosis warning.

#### Colorectal Neoplasia
- Colorectal tumors: up to **60%** of all GI neoplasms in dogs and cats.
- Adenocarcinoma, lymphoma, leiomyosarcoma, hemangiosarcoma, plasmacytomas.
- Miniature Dachshunds predisposed to adenomatous colonic polyps.
- Siamese cats predisposed to alimentary lymphoma.
- Feline large intestinal SCL (MST 18–30 months); LGL lymphoma (MST 21 days).

**> ClinIQ: ✓ CRC** — Colorectal Neoplasia covered.

### Ch 264 — Rectoanal Diseases

**Author:** Emily N. Gould

#### Perianal Fistula (PAF) / Canine Anal Furunculosis

**Signalment/Predisposition:**
- GSDs: up to **85%** of some PAF populations; genetic association with DLA-DRB1*00101 and CTNND2 gene.
- Mean age at presentation: **4–7 years**.
- Other breeds: Australian Shepherd, Beagle, Border Collie, Bouvier des Flandres, English Bulldog, Irish Setter, Labrador Retriever.

**Treatment:**
- **Cornerstone:** calcineurin inhibitors (cyclosporine A, tacrolimus) — specifically inhibit T-cell-mediated immunity.
- CsA monotherapy: **85% of GSDs achieved remission by 16 weeks** in randomized controlled trial; 0% of placebo group.
- Starting cyclosporine: **2–4 mg/kg/day CsA** combined with **5–10 mg/kg/day ketoconazole (KZ)** — currently acceptable starting regimen.
  - KZ reduces CsA requirement (inhibits P450 → increases CsA bioavailability), reducing cost and side-effects.
  - Escalate CsA dosage if no improvement in first 4–5 weeks.
  - Controlled studies: 65–100% of dogs achieve remission 3–16 weeks into CsA+KZ therapy.
  - Once clinical improvement: taper CsA by ~25% reduction every 4–8 weeks.
  - Microemulsified/modified CsA preferred (increased bioavailability); administer on empty stomach ≥2h before/after meal.
- Topical tacrolimus: **50% of dogs achieved clinical remission** in one study; twice daily; irritating to tissues, more suitable for smaller lesions.
- Prednisone: only ~33% remission rate (with novel protein diet in one study) — less favorable than CsA.
- Azathioprine: variable remission rates; MMF: no response in one report.
- Surgery: only if comorbidities (abscessation) exist OR after 6 weeks of medical therapy fails.
- Novel: local MSC therapy — 6/6 dogs with PAF refractory to ≥6 months CsA showed resolution of draining tracts 3 months post-injection; 2/6 had recurrence by 6 months.
- Diet: novel/hydrolyzed protein diet often recommended (CAFR may contribute to PAF development).

**Prognosis:** Variable; concurrent PHs or draining tracts communicating with anal canal → worse prognosis.

**> ClinIQ: ✗ MISSING** — Perianal Fistula/Anal Furunculosis (candidate for new ClinIQ entry — major disease with detailed treatment algorithm).

#### Anal Sac Disease (Non-Neoplastic)
- Third most common reason dogs presented to primary-care facilities (one UK study).
- Cavalier King Charles Spaniels, Labrador Retrievers, Chihuahuas, Miniature Poodles overrepresented.
- Treatment: flushing, warm compresses, local antibiotic instillation + topical corticosteroids; anal sacculectomy for recurrent sacculitis/abscessation.

**> ClinIQ: ✗ MISSING** — Anal Sac Disease (candidate).

#### AGASACA (Apocrine Gland Anal Sac Adenocarcinoma)
- <20% of perianal tumors; metastasis in **50–90%** at diagnosis; most common sites: regional LN → lungs → abdominal organs.
- Older female dogs possibly overrepresented; English Cocker Spaniel at higher risk (DLA-DQB1 allele association).
- Paraneoplastic hypercalcemia (PTHrP upregulation) → shorter MST in hypercalcemic dogs.
- **Prognosis:** MST 1–2 years with surgery ± chemo/radiation; early-stage (no metastasis) MST ~1200 days (40 months) with surgery alone.
- Negative prognostic indicators: sublumbar lymphadenopathy, lymph node extirpation, pulmonary metastasis, tumor size.
- **Postoperative calcium monitoring mandatory** if pre-op hypercalcemia (hypocalcemia reported post-AGASACA resection).
- Cats: AGASACA overall survival 89–169 days (less favorable than dogs).

**> ClinIQ: ✗ MISSING** — AGASACA (candidate — Apocrine Gland Anal Sac Adenocarcinoma).

#### Perianal Adenoma/Adenocarcinoma
- Adenomas: testosterone-driven; >80% of intact male dogs; castration curative in **90%** of cases.
- Adenocarcinoma: metastasis rate <15%; requires wide surgical margins; MST <1 year with tumors >5 cm.

### Ch 265 — Peritoneal Diseases

**Authors:** Genna Atiee, Kelley M. Thieman

#### Effusion Classification — Diagnostic Thresholds

**Septic Peritonitis:**
- **Intracellular bacteria on cytology** = diagnostic (Figure 265.3).
- Blood-to-fluid glucose (BFG) difference **>20 mg/dL (>1.1 mmol/L)**: 100% sensitive + 100% specific for septic peritonitis in dogs; 86% sensitive + 100% specific in cats (Ch 265, citing Bonczynski et al., Vet Surg 2003).
- Blood-to-fluid lactate (BFL) difference **< –2.0 mmol/L**: 100% sensitive + 100% specific for septic peritoneal effusion (Ch 265).

**Uroperitoneum:**
- Fluid creatinine **>2× serum creatinine**: confirmatory.
- Fluid potassium:serum potassium ratio **>1.4**: confirmatory.

**Bile Peritonitis:**
- Abdominal fluid bilirubin **>2× concurrent serum bilirubin concentration**: confirmatory.
- Golden-green granular pigment on cytology; or acellular mucinous fibrillar material.

**Hemorrhagic Effusion:**
- Fluid PCV **≥25%** of peripheral blood PCV.
- Hemorrhagic effusion should **not clot** even in a clot tube.

**Chylous Effusion:**
- Fluid triglyceride concentration **>serum triglyceride concentration**: definitive.

**General Effusion Classification:**
- TNCC <3,000/mcL = transudate; >3,000/mcL = exudate.
- Total protein cutoff: **2.5 g/dL (25 g/L)** — further classifies as low- vs. high-protein.
- Note: dogs with albumin <1.8 g/dL (<18 g/L) commonly have protein-poor transudates (contradicts popular teaching that serum protein must be <1.5 g/dL for effusion).

**> ClinIQ: ✗ MISSING** — Septic Peritonitis (candidate for new ClinIQ entry — critical disease with key diagnostic thresholds for BFG/BFL).

#### Survival Rates
- Secondary peritonitis (dogs): **32–54%** survival.
- Cats with septic peritonitis: **44–70%** survival.

#### Surgical Management
- Lavage volume: **200–300 mL/kg** warm sterile physiologic saline; continue until returning fluid is clear.
- Preoperative risk factors for tertiary peritonitis: preoperative septic peritonitis, low serum albumin/plasma protein, intraoperative hypotension.
- Uroperitoneum: not always surgical; non-septic uroabdomen = medical emergency (hyperkalemia) but not surgical emergency; urinary diversion (urethral catheterization, tube cystostomy) prior to repair.

#### Most Common Bacterial Isolates (Table 265.1)
- E. coli*, Enterococcus spp.*, Clostridium spp.* (most common); also Bacteroides, Fusobacterium, Actinomyces, Morganella morganii, Peptostreptococcus.
