### Ch 316 — Tumor Biology

**Key concepts (clinically applicable)**
- Cancer defined by 8 hallmarks (Hanahan & Weinberg 2011): sustaining proliferative signaling, evading growth suppressors, resisting cell death, enabling replicative immortality, inducing angiogenesis, activating invasion/metastasis, deregulating cellular metabolism, avoiding immune destruction.
- P53 loss is the most common genetic alteration across cancers (including veterinary tumors).
- Tumor angiogenesis driven by VEGF, PDGF, bFGF; toceranib (Palladia) targets VEGFR2, PDGFR-β, and KIT — relevant across many tumor types beyond MCT.
- Cancer stem cells (CSCs): slowly dividing, resistant to conventional therapy; first identified in AML (CD34+ cells); implicated in recurrence.

### Ch 317 — Principles and Practice of Chemotherapy

**Key clinical thresholds**
- Febrile neutropenia (FN) mortality in dogs: ~9%. Main risk factors: lymphoma, small body size, not in remission (66%), induction phase (72%), first dose of drug (54%), doxorubicin or vincristine.
- Neutrophil threshold to delay myelosuppressive chemotherapy: **<1500–2000 cells/mcL** (recheck in 5–7 days). If <1000/mcL with scheduled chemo, delay until >2000/mcL; threshold for antibiotics in subclinical neutropenia is <750–1000/mcL.
- Carboplatin dosing reduced when GFR reduced (creatinine elevated).
- Doxorubicin cardiac toxicity risk in dogs with no pre-existing heart disease: cumulative dose **>180 mg/m²**; echocardiographic changes seen as early as 30 mg/m².
- Response categories: CR (complete), PR (partial), SD (stable), PD (progressive). ORR = CR + PR.
- Cats: FN rare; vinca alkaloids and lomustine most common causes.
- Prophylactic TMS (trimethoprim-sulfa) during doxorubicin reduced hospitalisation risk in dogs with lymphoma/osteosarcoma (double-blind RCT).

**Drug classes**
- Alkylating agents: cyclophosphamide, chlorambucil, melphalan, lomustine (CCNU), dacarbazine, ifosfamide — low cross-resistance.
- Anthracyclines (antitumor antibiotics): doxorubicin, epirubicin, mitoxantrone, dactinomycin — MDR pump substrates; cross-resistance with mitotic inhibitors.
- Mitotic inhibitors (vinca alkaloids): vincristine (0.7 mg/m² IV dogs; 0.5–0.7 mg/m² cats), vinblastine, vinorelbine.
- Platinum drugs: cisplatin, carboplatin — no cross-resistance with other classes.
- Antimetabolites: rarely used due to high toxicity/low efficacy at veterinary dosages (exceptions: rabacfosadine, gemcitabine).
- TKIs (molecular targeted): ch. 320.

**Metronomic chemotherapy**: low-dose continuous cyclophosphamide (10 mg/m² PO q24–48h) + piroxicam (0.3 mg/kg PO q24h) — anti-angiogenic, Treg depletion, used for STS and HSA maintenance.

### Ch 318 — Principles and Practice of Radiation Oncology

**Radiobiology key numbers**
- 1 Gy x-ray → ~40 double-strand breaks (DSBs), 1000 single-strand breaks, >2000 base damages.
- Repair of sublethal damage: 6–24 hours. Normal cells repair more efficiently than tumor cells.
- Radiosensitive phases: M and G2 (most sensitive); S and G0 (most resistant).
- Late effects defined as >90 days post-RT; tissues: heart, lung, muscle, bone, nerves.
- Secondary cancer from RT in dogs: <3.5%.

**Clinical RT applications (selected)**
- Nasal neoplasia (dogs): definitive RT = standard of care; median survival ~14 months. Palliative RT: most achieve partial/complete resolution of clinical signs. (Ch 318)
- Oral neoplasia: melanoma response rate up to 80–90%.
- Brain tumors: definitive RT → median survival ~19 months. SRT survival similar.
- Pituitary tumors (dogs): radiation survival ~2 years; feline acromegaly SRT → median survival ~35 months.
- Soft-tissue sarcomas (dogs): definitive post-excision RT → MSTs 60 months to not reached; recurrence rate 17–31.4%.
- MCT: 2-year control after incomplete excision + definitive RT = 85–95%.
- Osteosarcoma (palliative RT): 80–90% achieve pain relief, typically 3–4 months duration.
- Osteosarcoma (SRT): pathological fracture rate 41–63%.
- Thymoma: radiation-sensitive; used when surgery not possible.
- TCC: definitive IMRT + medical therapy = 61% overall response rate.
- Anal sac adenocarcinoma: palliative or definitive RT described.

> ClinIQ: ✓ Nasal Neoplasia (DIS-NASAL-NEO), Intracranial Neoplasia (DIS-BRAIN-NEO), Spinal Meningioma (DIS-NEU-SPINEO), Thymoma (DIS-RESP-THYMOMA) — enrichment: radiation survival data as above.

### Ch 319 — Cancer Immunotherapy

**Key points**
- Oncept (canine tyrosinase DNA vaccine): approved for oral melanoma in dogs; evidence conflicting but some long-term survivors reported.
- Toceranib, losartan, propranolol: immunomodulatory/MDSC-depleting properties beyond direct anti-tumor action.
- Checkpoint inhibitors (anti-PD1, anti-PD-L1, anti-CTLA-4): canine trials underway; melanoma responds best (including pulmonary metastasis). "Hot" tumors (melanoma, urothelial, high-grade sarcomas) more likely to respond; "cold" tumors (OSA, glioma) unlikely.
- CAR T cells: CD19/CD20 canine CAR T cell trials in lymphoma: safety established; long-term remission not yet achieved.
- L-MTP-PE (liposomal muramyl tripeptide): demonstrated improved disease-free and overall survival in OSA when combined with AC chemotherapy — no longer clinically available.

> ClinIQ: No new disease entity; reference chapter.

### Ch 320 — Molecular Targeted Therapy

**Toceranib phosphate (TOC; Palladia, Zoetis) — DOGS**
- Approved: recurrent Patnaik grade 2 and 3 cutaneous MCT.
- Mechanism: multikinase inhibitor (KIT, VEGFR2, PDGFR-β).
- Standard dose: **2.4–2.9 mg/kg PO q48h** or Mon/Wed/Fri; maximum tolerated dose 3.25 mg/kg q48h.
- Dose reduction if AE: reduce by 0.25–0.5 mg/kg; maintain ≥2.4 mg/kg for efficacy; or change to 3×/week.
- MCT registration study (n=145, grade 2–3 unresectable): RR 42.8% (CR 14.5%, PR 28.3%); biologic RR 60% (adding SD). c-KIT mutation-positive MCTs: RR ~69% vs. ~37% wild-type.
- Neutropenia: suspend if <1500/mcL; resume with dose reduction.
- Common AEs: GI toxicosis (anorexia, weight loss, nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, GI bleeding), myelotoxicosis, musculoskeletal (lameness — usually mild, resolves with drug holiday + NSAIDs/tramadol). Less common: depigmentation, hypertension, hypothyroidism (long-term), PLN, pancreatitis.
- Off-label use: anal sac adenocarcinoma, thyroid carcinoma, nasal carcinoma, chemodectoma, bladder tumor, many adenocarcinomas. See Table 320.1 for RR/CB by tumor type.

**CATS**
- Used at 2.5–2.8 mg/kg PO q48h or 3×/week. GI AEs less common vs. dogs; hepatotoxicity grade 3–4 in small percentage.
- Direct action demonstrated in feline MCT (c-KIT mutations in exon 5).
- Response in feline oral SCC when combined with NSAIDs.

**Masitinib (MAS; Masivet, AB Science)**
- Approved Europe 2008: Patnaik grade 2 or 3 canine MCT.
- Dose: 12.5 mg/kg PO q24h (10–15 mg/kg for adequate plasma concentrations).
- PLN as notable AE; hemolytic anemia rare.

**Imatinib (Gleevec, Novartis)**
- Dogs and cats: 10 mg/kg PO q24h. Targets MCT and GIST; milder AEs than TOC. Nephrotoxicosis in cats.

**Verdinexor (Laverdia-CA1, Anivive)**
- FDA conditionally approved January 2021 for canine LSA.
- Phase 2 (n=58 dogs): 37% RR overall, 71% RR for T-cell LSA. 95% of AEs grade 1–2.

> ClinIQ: ✓ Haemangiosarcoma (DIS-NEO-HSA) — TOC efficacy limited; ✓ Mediastinal Lymphoma (DIS-RESP-MEDLYM) — verdinexor applies. ✗ MISSING: MCT (primary page, DIS-NEO-MCT candidate), GIST (DIS-GI-GIST candidate).

### Ch 321 — Complications of Cancer Therapy

**Cytopenia grading (VCOG-CTCAE)**

| Grade | Neutropenia (/mcL) | Thrombocytopenia (k/mcL) | Anemia PCV (dog/cat) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1500 to <LLN | 100 to <LLN | 30%/<LLN or 25%/<LLN |
| 2 | 1000–1499 | 50–99 | 20–30% / 20–25% |
| 3 | 500–999 | 25–49 | 15–20% / 15–20% |
| 4 | <499 | <25 | <15% / <15% |

**GI toxicity grading (VCOG-CTCAE)**
- Vomiting grade 1: <3 episodes/24h; grade 2: 3–10 episodes or ≤48h IV fluids; grade 3: multiple episodes >48h requiring IV fluids/PPN; grade 4: life-threatening.
- Diarrhea grade 1: ≤2 extra stools/day; grade 2: 3–6 extra stools; grade 3: >6 extra stools, incontinence >48h, IV fluids; grade 4: hemodynamic collapse.

**Hepatotoxicity (CCNU most significant)**
- CCNU: ALT increases in up to 86% of treated dogs; cumulative, often irreversible. Delay/discontinue if >3× ULN. Add hepatoprotectants (S-adenosylmethionine + silybin = Denamarin) — shown to reduce rate/severity of enzyme elevations in RCT.
- ALT grading dogs: grade 1 = >ULN to 1.5×; grade 2 = >1.5–4×; grade 3 = >4–10×; grade 4 = >10×.

**Nadir timing**
- Vinca alkaloids, doxorubicin, mitoxantrone, RAB, alkylators: neutrophil nadir ~7 days (range 5–10).
- Carboplatin: nadir 14–21 days in dogs and cats.
- CCNU in cats: nadir 7–28 days, persists up to 14 days.

**Breed MDR-1 risk**: Collies (rough/smooth), Australian Shepherds, McNabs, Silken Windhounds, Long-Haired Whippets, Shetland Sheepdogs, German Shepherds — higher toxicity with vincristine, doxorubicin, mitoxantrone, taxanes, dactinomycin.

**Doxorubicin cardiotoxicity**: cumulative dose threshold >180 mg/m² in dogs without pre-existing cardiac disease; fractional shortening decreases as early as 30 mg/m². Dexrazoxane (10× doxorubicin dose IV) for cardioprotection or extravasation management (give within 3 hours of extravasation; repeat at 24 and 48h).

**Sterile hemorrhagic cystitis (SHC)**: cyclophosphamide or ifosfamide → acrolein metabolite. Furosemide significantly reduces SHC risk with high-dose cyclophosphamide; mesna required with ifosfamide. Metronomic cyclophosphamide + furosemide: SHC rate 3.6%.

**Cisplatin absolute contraindication in cats** (fatal pulmonary edema). 5-FU: fatal neurotoxicosis in cats (never use). Chlorambucil: reversible myoclonus/seizures in cats (especially pulse dosing).

**Doxorubicin in cats**: safe at 22–25 mg/m²; nephrotoxic at dog dosages. Monitor renal parameters.

### Ch 322 — Hematopoietic Tumors

#### LYMPHOMA (LSA)

**ClinIQ: ✓ Gastric Lymphoma (DIS-GI-LYMP), Mediastinal Lymphoma (DIS-RESP-MEDLYM) — enrichments below. ✗ MISSING: Multicentric Canine Lymphoma (primary page), Feline Lymphoma (primary page), Multiple Myeloma.**

**WHO Clinical Staging for Domestic Animals with Lymphoma**

| Stage | Criteria |
|---|---|
| I | Single lymph node |
| II | Multiple LNs, regional (same side of diaphragm) |
| III | Generalized lymphadenopathy |
| IV | Liver and/or spleen involvement (± LN) |
| V | Any other site, including bone marrow and blood |
| Substage a | Without clinical signs |
| Substage b | With clinical signs |

**Histopathologic classifications — Dogs**
- DLBCL: ~50% of cases; large B-cell; characterized by large lymphoid cells effacing LNs.
- PTCL (peripheral T-cell lymphoma): ~15%.
- T-zone lymphoma (TZL): ~10–15%; indolent; cytology shows "uropod" or "mirror handle" cytoplasmic extension; CD3+, CD5+, CD45−, class II MHC high.
- Marginal zone LSA: 5–15%.
- Grade: low (MI 0–5/400× field), intermediate (MI 6–10), high (MI >10).

**Histopathologic classifications — Cats**
- GI tract most common site (~all forms).
- Mucosal T-cell LSA (EATCL type II): small-to-intermediate lymphocytes, lamina propria, patchy/diffuse; treated with chlorambucil/prednisolone.
- Transmural T-cell LSA (EATCL type I): large granular lymphocytes; focal/multifocal small intestine; aggressive.
- DLBCL: transmural large lymphocytes; stomach, jejunum, ileocecocolic junction.
- T-cell rich B-cell lymphoma (TCRBCL, formerly Hodgkin's-like): ~10% of all feline LSA; cervical/mandibular LNs; slow progression.

**Diagnostic criteria**
- Histopathology with immunophenotyping: gold standard.
- DLBCL/PTCL cytology: large lymphoid cells with fine chromatin, multiple peripheral nucleoli, deep blue cytoplasm.
- TZL cytology: small-to-intermediate cells with uropod extensions.
- Large granular lymphocyte LSA cytology (cats): large azurophilic magenta granules — Wright-Giemsa required (NOT Diff-Quik).
- Flow cytometry: B vs T phenotype (95% agreement with IHC). B-cell LSA negative prognostic factors: large cell size, low MHC class II expression, high CD25. TZL: CD3+, CD5+, CD45−. PTCL: CD45+, low MHC class II; most CD4+, CD8−.
- PARR (PCR for antigen receptor rearrangement): sensitivity 70–85% (dogs), 60–65% (cats); specificity >95%. False positives in ehrlichiosis. Applicable to formalin-fixed, EDTA blood, air-dried cytology, CSF (only ~50,000 cells needed).

**CBC abnormalities in LSA**
- Anemia: 30–40% dogs, 45–60% cats (negative prognostic factor).
- Thrombocytopenia: 20–50% dogs, 10–20% cats.
- Hypercalcemia: 35–55% of dogs with **T-cell LSA** (PTHrP-mediated; negative prognostic factor). Less common in cats.

- Indolent LSA (TZL): chlorambucil 4 mg/m² PO q24h (or 20 mg/m² PO q2wk) + prednisone; MST >2 years common.
- Cutaneous epitheliotropic LSA: CCNU 70 mg/m² PO q3wk + prednisone; ORR ~80% (mostly PR); median remission ~3 months.

#### LEUKEMIA

**CLL**
- Dogs: ≥75% CD8+ CD45+ T-cell; >50% with azurophilic granules (LGLs). CD21+ B-cell CLL is next most common. ~30% of Golden Retrievers >10 years have T-zone-like cells of undetermined significance on flow cytometry.
- Cats: almost all CD4+ T-cell; ~50% incidental diagnosis.
- Treat when: lymphocyte count ≥30,000/mcL, organ involvement beyond mild LN/spleen, cytopenias, or clinical signs affecting QoL.
- Treatment: chlorambucil + prednisone/prednisolone (same as indolent LSA protocols). MST 12–18 months (dogs and cats).

**Acute Leukemia (ALL/AML)**
- Diagnosis: >20% blasts in peripheral blood or BM (older studies: >30%).
- CD34+ distinguishes acute leukemia from LSA (but up to 25% of canine acute leukemias are CD34−).
- Lineage: ~50–55% ALL, ~45–50% AML in dogs (by flow cytometry/IHC).
- ALL treatment: L-CHOP (CHOP + L-asparaginase at induction — crucial as does not impair hematopoiesis).
- Prognosis: grave. MST <3 months; survival >6 months rare in dogs and cats.
- T-cell ALL: can cause paraneoplastic hypercalcemia.

#### MULTIPLE MYELOMA (MM)

**ClinIQ: ✗ MISSING (candidate: DIS-NEO-MYELOMA)**

**Diagnostic criteria (≥2 of 4 required)**:
1. BM plasmacytosis **>20%**
2. Serum monoclonal gammopathy (M component)
3. Osteolytic bone lesions
4. Bence-Jones proteinuria (light chains in urine)

*Author preference: BM plasmacytosis + at least 1 other criterion.*

**Cats**: BM involvement not consistent; modified criteria include plasma cell morphology + visceral organ involvement. Monoclonal gammopathy alone is insufficient for MM diagnosis (DDx: B-cell LSA, B-cell CLL, ehrlichiosis, leishmaniasis, FIP).

**Clinical signs**
- Lethargy, weakness, decreased appetite.
- Osteolytic bone lesions: vertebrae, ribs, pelvis, long bones → bone pain, pathological fractures, spinal cord compression.
- Paraneoplastic hypercalcemia (bone lysis > PTHrP mechanism in MM).
- M component → hyperviscosity syndrome: retinal vascular changes (enlarged/tortuous vessels, hemorrhage, retinal detachment → acute-onset blindness); neurologic signs (stupor, ataxia, seizures); cardiomegaly, CHF.
- Bence-Jones proteinuria → renal tubular casts, interstitial nephritis, renal failure.
- Bleeding diathesis (platelet coating by M component → functional thrombopathy → epistaxis, petechiae).
- Secondary infections (normal Ig suppressed).

**Treatment — Dogs**
- Melphalan + prednisone (two protocols):
  - Protocol 1: **melphalan 0.1 mg/kg PO q24h × 10 days, then 0.05 mg/kg PO q24h indefinitely** + prednisone 0.5 mg/kg PO q24h × 10d, then 0.5 mg/kg PO q48h × 60d.
  - Protocol 2: **melphalan 7 mg/m² PO q24h × 5 days q3wk** + same prednisone.
  - Single dose cyclophosphamide (200–250 mg/m² IV or PO) sometimes added at induction for severe signs.
- Response: 85–90% have improvement in serum globulins. MST 1.5–2.5 years.
- Negative prognostic factors: hypercalcemia, extensive lytic bone lesions, Bence-Jones proteinuria.
- Zoledronate: reduces hypercalcemia, alleviates bone pain, helps prevent pathological fractures.

**Treatment — Cats**
- Melphalan avoided due to idiosyncratic severe refractory myelosuppression.
- Cyclophosphamide 200 mg/m² IV or PO q2–3wk (or 25 mg/cat PO twice a week) + prednisolone 1 mg/kg PO q24h × 14d, then q48h indefinitely; or COP protocol.
- Response: 50–85% positive; MST 8–13 months.
- Negative prognostic factors (cats): hypercalcemia, bony lesions with pathological fracture, anemia, Bence-Jones proteinuria, azotemia, <8 weeks improvement in serum proteins.

**Hyperviscosity management**: plasmapheresis most effective.

**Solitary osseous plasmacytoma**: strong staging for systemic MM required; RT highly effective for local control. Most progress to MM (months to years). Canine cutaneous extramedullary plasmacytoma: 5% local recurrence, 2% metastatic rate; surgery usually curative.

### Ch 323 — Tumors of the Skin

**ClinIQ: ✓ Otic Neoplasia (DIS-NEU-OTIC-NEO) — ceruminous gland tumors; ✗ MISSING (candidates): Cutaneous MCT → Ch 327; Cutaneous Melanoma; Cutaneous SCC; Plasmacytoma; Histiocytoma.**

**Prevalence**
- Skin tumors: ~25–45% of canine, ~20–30% of feline neoplasms.
- Dogs: ~57–80% benign.
- Cats: ~50–65% malignant.
- Top 4 canine skin tumors: soft tissue sarcoma (17.7%), MCT (16%), lipoma (11%), histiocytoma (9.1%).
- Top 4 feline skin tumors: basal cell tumor (26.7%), fibrosarcoma (19.1%), SCC (11.5%), MCT (7.4%).

**Canine cutaneous melanoma — prognostic factors**
- Negative: advanced clinical stage, high mitotic count (≥3 or >20/10 HPF), high Ki-67/MIB-1 (≥15%), necrosis, ulceration, deep (non-dermal) location, lack of pigmentation, junctional activity, older age (>9.4 years).
- Surgical margins: not an important prognostic factor for survival in purely cutaneous melanoma (unlike oral).
- Metastasis: up to 22%; recurrence 8% at median 9 months post-surgery.
- Treatment: surgery; carboplatin ± Oncept (xenogeneic DNA tyrosinase vaccine); coarse-fractionated RT for unresectable.
- Feline cutaneous melanoma: MST ~4.5 months after surgery; 48.9% develop local recurrence or metastasis.

**Canine squamous cell carcinoma (dermal)**
- Metastatic rate: 5–22% (predominantly regional LN, rarely lungs).
- Solar-induced actinic changes: ~40% of affected dogs.
- Negative prognostic factors: non-actinic dermal SCC, non-predisposed breeds, white coat dogs, ventral cervical location, nodal/distant metastasis.
- Treatment options for solar lesions/pre-neoplastic: piroxicam, retinoids, imiquimod, strontium-90 plesiotherapy, intralesional chemotherapy.

**Feline SCC**: staging by clinical stage is most reliable prognostic factor. Actinically induced (nasal planum, pinnae, eyelids in white/partly white cats).

**Bowenoid in situ carcinoma (BISC, cats)**: multicentric, crusty/waxy plaques, not sun-related (papillomavirus). ~20% progress to invasive SCC. Devon Rex cats predisposed to aggressive early-onset BISC.

**Basal cell tumors (BCT, cats)**: most common feline skin tumor; typically benign; head; surgery curative; metastasis not reported.

**Ceruminous gland tumors**: TECA-LBO significantly lowers recurrence and increases remission duration vs. lateral ear canal resection for carcinomas.

**Feline injection-site 3-2-1 rule**: biopsy if mass persists >3 months post-vaccination, >2 cm in diameter, or increasing in size >1 month after injection.

### Ch 324 — Soft Tissue Sarcomas

**ClinIQ: ✓ PNST (DIS-PNST) — included in STS spectrum. ✗ MISSING: Soft Tissue Sarcoma (primary page), Feline Injection-Site Sarcoma, GI Stromal Tumor (GIST).**

**Grading System (Table 324.1)**

| Score | Differentiation | Mitoses (/10 HPF at 400×) | Necrosis |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Resembles normal mesenchymal tissue | 0–9 | None |
| 2 | Histologic type determinable | 10–19 | ≤50% |
| 3 | Unknown histologic type/undifferentiated | >19 | >50% |

| Histologic Grade | Total Score |
|---|---|
| I | ≤4 |
| II | 5 or 6 |
| III | ≥7 |

**Prognosis by grade**
- Risk of recurrence (margins <1 mm): grade I <10%; grade II 30%; grade III 75%.
- Metastatic rate: grade I 0–13%; grade II 5–20%; grade III 40%.
- Mitotic index (MI) <9: lower recurrence risk even with narrow margins.
- MI >19: increased metastatic risk and shorter survival.
- Overall: most dogs treated for STS do not die of disease. MST with surgery ± RT: 2–6 years; ~1/3 die of tumor-related causes.

**Surgical margin guidelines**
- Complete excision: >3–5 mm histologic margin.
- Narrow/marginal: <3 mm, no cells at edge.
- Re-excision preferred over RT if feasible (0.5 cm gross margin achievable).
- Definitive post-excision RT: 15–25 fractions, total 50–60 Gy; MSTs 60 months to not reached.

**Feline Injection-Site Sarcoma (ISS)**
- Incidence: 1–100 per 10,000 vaccinated cats.
- 3-2-1 rule for biopsy (see Ch 323).
- Most locally invasive compared to non-vaccine STS; microscopic extensions far beyond palpable mass.
- Metastasis: 10–25% of cats, usually lungs and LNs; fatal in 20–50%.
- Radical excision with **5 cm** lateral margins + ≥2 fascial planes deep: lowest recurrence rate (~14%).
- Wide excision (3 cm lateral, body wall resection, 2 fascial planes): recurrence rate 38%; median time to recurrence 1.75 years.
- Surgery + definitive RT (48–57 Gy daily × ~4 weeks): MST 1.5–3.5 years; preoperative RT preferred.
- Vaccination site recommendations: below right elbow (core vaccines), below right stifle (rabies), below left stifle (FeLV), distal tail. Avoid interscapular region and proximal limbs.

**GIST (dogs)**
- Immunophenotype: CD117+ (c-KIT), DOG1+, SMA ± (vs. LMSA: CD117−, DOG1−, SMA+).
- Surgical: 5 cm margin on either side of GI tract; complete excision potentially curative.
- Toceranib for unresectable/metastatic GIST: >50% clinical benefit; median time to progression ~2 years (post-clinical benefit); 1.25 years in microscopic disease setting.
- Prognosis (GIST and LMSA): MST 8 months to 2.8 years; longer with complete excision and MI <9–10 (MST 3.5+ years vs. 6–10 months with MI ≥9–10).

### Ch 325 — Hemangiosarcoma

**ClinIQ: ✓ Haemangiosarcoma (DIS-NEO-HSA) — major enrichment below.**

**"Rule of two-thirds" (splenic masses in dogs)**
- Two-thirds of dogs with a splenic mass have a malignancy.
- Two-thirds of malignant splenic tumors are HSA.

*Note: This is an informal pathology observation — no patient with hemoabdomen should be euthanized based on identifying a solitary splenic mass "consistent with HSA" on US. Many hematomas appear similar.*

**TNM Clinical Staging System (Box 325.1)**

| Stage | TNM Criteria |
|---|---|
| I | T0 or T1, N0, M0 |
| II | T1 or T2, N0 or N1 |
| III | T2 or T3, N0/N1/N2, M1 |

T1 = tumor confined to primary site ± dermis, <5 cm; T2 = SQ invasion or ≥5 cm; T3 = invades adjacent structures/muscle; N0 = no regional LN; N1 = regional LN; N2 = distant LN; M0 = no distant metastasis; M1 = distant metastasis.

**Diagnosis**
- Cytology: low diagnostic yield for HSA (diagnostic in some SQ/IM tumors).
- Histology: irregular anastomosing vascular channels, pleomorphic spindle cells, blood-filled cavities, necrosis, high MI.
- IHC: von Willebrand factor (factor VIII–related antigen) or CD31 confirm endothelial origin.
- Thrombocytopenia: up to 75% of dogs with HSA (negative prognostic indicator); DIC in up to 50%.
- cTnI: cardiac HSA detection — sensitivity 78%, specificity 71% vs. other sites; sensitivity 81%, specificity 100% vs. pericardial effusion not from HSA.

**Surgery outcomes**
- Dermal HSA (no hypodermal invasion): MST 780–987 days with excision alone; 77% locoregional recurrence.
- SQ HSA: MST ~172 days; IM HSA: MST ~307 days — both require aggressive surgery + adjuvant chemotherapy.
- Visceral (splenic) HSA — splenectomy alone: MST 19–86 days (dogs); ~77 days (cats, laparotomy).
- Cardiac HSA — surgery alone: MST ~42 days; surgery + chemotherapy: MST ~175 days. Single-agent DOX for cardiac HSA: objective RR 41%, clinical benefit 68%, MST 116 days (vs. 12 days untreated).

**Chemotherapy**
- Doxorubicin (DOX): most active agent; MST 5–7 months with DOX-based protocols post-splenectomy.
- AC protocol (DOX + cyclophosphamide): MST ~142 days.
- DOX/DTIC protocol: MST >550 days in small cohort (9 dogs — limited conclusions).
- Carboplatin alternative: comparable MSTs to DOX (160 vs. 139 days) in small study.
- VAC (vincristine/Adriamycin/cyclophosphamide): overall MST 189 days for stage I–III HSA; objective RR 86% in those assessed.
- Metronomic cyclophosphamide + etoposide + piroxicam: pilot study MST 178 days (vs. 133 with DOX alone, stage II).
- L-MTP-PE + AC (no longer available): MST 277 days (vs. placebo); especially stage I.

> **Standard of care**: splenectomy (or applicable surgery) + adjuvant DOX-based chemotherapy.

### Ch 326 — Primary Bone Tumors in Dogs

**ClinIQ: ✗ MISSING (candidate: DIS-NEO-OSA — Osteosarcoma).**

**Osteosarcoma (OSA)**
- Breed: large/giant breeds; median age 7–10 years.
- Most common sites: distal radius, proximal humerus (distal femur, proximal tibia less frequent).
- Hallmarks: cortical lysis, periosteal reaction, extension into soft tissue, loss of fine trabecular pattern, areas of fine punctuate lysis, lack of distinct border between normal/abnormal bone — vs. sharp demarcation (suggests infection or bone cyst).

**Cytology/biopsy**
- FNA first; sensitivity 97%, specificity 100% for OSA in one study; ALP staining sensitivity 88–100%, specificity 94%.
- Bone biopsy (Jamshidi): 91.9% discrimination of tumor vs. non-tumor; 82.3% correct tumor type.
- Biopsy tract considered contaminated with tumor cells.

**Staging**
- <10% of dogs with OSA have gross pulmonary disease on thoracic radiographs at diagnosis.
- Bone metastasis: CT more sensitive; bone scan (~7.8% OSA have suspected bone metastasis); PET-CT emerging tool.
- Lymph node metastasis rate: 4.4%; dogs with positive LN have MST 59 days vs. 318 days.
- Abdominal US rarely helpful unless palpable mass; metastasis to abdominal organs 0–2.5%.

**Negative prognostic factors**
- Elevated serum alkaline phosphatase (SAP): strongest negative prognostic indicator for both DFI and OST (meta-analyses).
- Older age (negative for OST).
- Site: proximal humerus (negative); distal radius possibly better.
- Chemotherapy after amputation improves OST.

**Treatment**
- Amputation + chemotherapy: standard of care; MST ~10–11 months; 1-year survival 36–48%; 2-year survival 18–22%. "Cure" in ~20% of dogs.
- Single-agent carboplatin (6 doses q3wk) now preferred over alternating carboplatin/doxorubicin (MST 425 vs. 135 days in RCT; lower AE rate).
- Palliative RT: 8–10 Gy weekly × 2–4 fractions; pain relief in 74–93%; duration 53–180 days.
- SRT (curative-intent non-surgical limb salvage): pathological fracture rate 41–63%; MST 233 days (123 dogs retrospective); dogs receiving salvage amputation after fracture had longer survival.
- Pamidronate: 1–2 mg/kg IV monthly; decreases pain; did not significantly change pain duration in placebo-controlled RT trial; avoid combining with palliative RT (negative survival effect in one study).

**Feline OSA**
- Metastatic rate much lower than canine (~10%); MST post-amputation 16.7–64 months without chemotherapy. Amputation without adjuvant chemotherapy recommended. Axial OSA worse prognosis than appendicular.

**Chondrosarcoma (CSA)**
- Much lower metastatic rate vs. OSA. MST with amputation alone: 979 days overall. Grade I: MST ~6 years; grade II: ~2.7 years; grade III: ~0.9 years. Chemotherapy value unknown for high-grade CSA.

### Ch 327 — Mast Cell Disease

**ClinIQ: ✗ MISSING (candidate: DIS-NEO-MCT — Mast Cell Tumor).**

**Epidemiology**
- Most common cutaneous malignancy in dogs: 16–21% of all cutaneous tumors.
- Predisposed breeds: English Bulldog, Boxer, Pug, Boston Terrier (brachycephalic — often low-grade), Schnauzers, retrievers, Cocker Spaniels, Beagles, Staffordshire Bull Terriers, Rhodesian Ridgebacks, Weimaraners, Chinese Shar-Peis (may be more aggressive).
- c-KIT mutations: 15–35% of intermediate/high-grade canine MCTs; exons 11, 8, 9. ITD exon 11 = high recurrence/metastasis; ITD exon 8 = possible long-term survival.
- Aberrant (cytoplasmic) KIT localization: associated with worse outcomes.

**Patnaik Grading (3-tier)**
- Grade I: well-differentiated (low-grade).
- Grade II: intermediate.
- Grade III: poorly differentiated (high-grade).
- Completely excised grade I/II without LN involvement: recurrence/metastasis risk ~10%. Completely excised grade III: low recurrence risk but high metastasis risk and short MST.

**Kiupel 2-tier Grading**
- Low grade vs. high grade based on 4 objective histologic features: mitotic count, multinucleation, bizarre nuclei, karyomegaly. Validated to be somewhat better at predicting MCT-related death.

**Mitotic Index (MI)**
- Strong independent predictor of outcome.
- MI <9: lower recurrence risk.
- High MI may predict shorter survival regardless of grade.

**Prognostic factors summary (Table 327.1)**
- Histologic grade: high-grade → increased recurrence AND metastasis risk.
- Location: subungual, oral, mucosal sites → more high-grade, worse prognosis; visceral → very poor; SQ → generally good.
- c-kit mutation: worse prognosis.
- KIT localization: non-membranous (aberrant) → worse prognosis.
- Clinical stage: regional LN involvement is negative factor.
- Breed: Boxers/brachycephalics → often low-grade → better prognosis.

**Staging**
- Before surgery (staging required unless low-risk, node-negative): CBC, serum biochemistry, cytologic LN eval, abdominal US, ± thoracic radiographs.
- LN: cytology FNA regional LN (including palpably normal nodes for melanoma/MCT); occasional solitary MCs in LN = normal (not diagnostic of metastasis); MC clustering/aggregates more worrisome.
- Grade scheme for LN infiltration: quantifies degree of involvement.
- Bone marrow: rarely informative (primary MC leukemia exceedingly rare).

**Surgical treatment**
- Wide excision: 3 cm lateral margin + 1 uninvolved fascial plane traditionally recommended; recent evidence: 1–2 cm lateral + 1 uninvolved fascial plane sufficient for many lower-grade small tumors.
- Incomplete excision: re-excision or adjuvant RT (2-year control rate 85–95% after incomplete excision + definitive RT).

**Tigilanol tiglate (TT)**
- Intralesional injection for non-metastatic cutaneous MCTs ≤10 cm³ volume at or below elbow/hock.
- CR rate: 75% after 1 injection, 88% after 2nd injection; 89% of CRs recurrence-free at 6 and 12 months.
- Wound necrosis expected in 3–14 days; heals ~4–6 weeks.

**Chemotherapy for high-risk/metastatic MCT (dogs)**
- First-line: vinblastine (VBL) + lomustine (CCNU) with corticosteroids (most commonly used).
- Overall objective RR with cytotoxic protocols: up to 64%; multi-agent > single-agent.
- Postoperative adjuvant VBL/prednisone or CCNU/prednisone for high-risk grade: median survival >2 years in most studies.
- TOC + VBL combination: 71% objective RR but significant myelosuppression (VBL dose/frequency reductions required).
- TOC + prednisone + hypofractionated RT (measurable MCTs): RR 76.4% (CR 58.8%, PR 17.6%); median PFI 10.5 months.
- TOC registration MCT study: RR 37.2% (CR 7, PR 25) vs. 7.9% placebo; median response duration 12 weeks; time to progression 18.1 weeks.

**Ancillary therapy**: H1 (diphenhydramine) + H2 (famotidine) blockers; omeprazole (PPI) — particularly for dogs with systemic signs, large tumor burden, or gross disease during treatment.

**Feline MCT**
- Three syndromes: cutaneous, splenic/visceral, intestinal.
- Cutaneous: most easily diagnosed by FNA; histiocytic form may regress spontaneously over 4–24 months; Oriental breeds predisposed.
- Splenic MCT: most common splenic differential in cats (15% of submissions); peripheral blood mastocytosis in most; splenectomy treatment of choice even with other organ involvement; MST 12–19 months.
- Intestinal MCT: 3rd most common feline GI tumor; aggressive; surgery + chemotherapy; poor prognosis.
- c-KIT mutations in exon 5 in ~67% of feline cutaneous and splenic/visceral MCTs.
- TOC response in feline MCT: ~70% objective RR.
- Feline MCT grading system validated 2019; has prognostic value.

### Ch 328 — Canine and Feline Histiocytic Diseases

**ClinIQ: ✗ MISSING (candidates): Histiocytic Sarcoma (DIS-NEO-HS), Histiocytoma, Systemic Histiocytosis.**

**Histiocytic Sarcoma (HS)**
- Breed predisposition: Bernese Mountain Dog, Flat-Coated Retriever (responsible for many deaths in these breeds), Rottweiler, Golden Retriever, Labrador Retriever.
- Periarticular HS: most common at stifle (37–61%); 55% have history of CCL rupture in affected joint; also Pembrook Welsh Corgis for CNS HS.
- Classification: localized HS (single organ) vs. disseminated HS (multisystem; formerly "malignant histiocytosis").
- Hemophagocytic HS: macrophage origin; CD11d+/CD18+; distinguished by marked erythrophagocytosis → regenerative hemolytic anemia (94% of dogs); thrombocytopenia (88%), hypoalbuminemia (94%), hypocholesterolemia (69%); aggressive; median time from onset to death/euthanasia 4 weeks.

**Diagnosis of HS**
- CD204 (macrophage scavenger receptor): highly specific IHC marker for histiocytic malignancies; applicable to cytologic and formalin-fixed tissues.
- CD11c+/CD18+: consistent for DC-origin HS. CD11d+/CD18+: hemophagocytic HS (macrophage origin).
- PTPN11 mutations: recurrent in visceral/disseminated canine HS and subset of human HS.

**Treatment**
- CCNU (lomustine): most effective single agent; **60–90 mg/m² PO q21 days**; response rate 29–46%; median response duration 90 days (range 29–805).
- Thrombocytopenia (<100,000/mcL) and hypoalbuminemia: negative prognostic factors with CCNU (MST <1 month if present).
- Localized HS + surgery/RT + adjuvant CCNU: median DFI 8 months; overall MST 18.6 months.
- Periarticular HS (n=49): surgery MST 13 months; RT MST 8 months (not significantly different).
- Pulmonary HS + surgery + adjuvant CCNU: overall MST 12.3 months.
- Alternating doxorubicin + CCNU: ORR 58%; median time to progression 185 days.
- Hemophagocytic HS: no effective treatment; CCNU may be considered but Flat-Coated Retrievers had median survival 24 days on CCNU.

### Ch 329 — Mammary Gland Tumors

**ClinIQ: ✗ MISSING (candidate: DIS-NEO-MGT — Mammary Gland Tumor).**

**Risk factors and spay timing**
- Dogs: spayed before 1st estrus = 0.5% risk; after 1st estrus = 8%; after 2nd estrus = 26%. After 3rd estrus / ≥4 years: OHE provides only modest protection. OHE at time of MGT removal reduces future MGT risk by ~50%.
- Cats: spayed before 1st estrus = 91% risk reduction; before 2nd = 86%; before 3rd = 11%. After 2 years = no protection.

**WHO TNM staging (modified)**
- Cats and dogs: Stage I (<2 cm), Stage II (2–3 cm), Stage III (>3 cm).

**Histologic types and behavior**
- Dogs: ~50–60% benign (fibroadenomas most common). Most common malignancies: solid carcinoma, tubular adenocarcinoma. ~3–5% sarcomas; ~1% inflammatory mammary carcinoma (IMGC).
- Of malignant MGTs: 50% recur, metastasize, or develop new tumors.
- Mortality: >40% of dogs/cats with invasive mammary carcinoma die within 1 year.
- Triple-negative tumors: ~58.6% of canine invasive MC; significantly shorter DFI and OS vs. luminal A.
- IMGC: rapidly growing, painful, high metastatic rate; DO NOT PERFORM SURGERY (high local recurrence within days; systemic disease even without detectable metastasis).

**Negative prognostic factors (dogs)**
- Histologic grade, stage, tumor size >3 cm, LN involvement, lymphovascular invasion, OHE status, estrogen/progesterone receptor expression, COX-2 expression, proliferation markers, growth pattern/fixation/ulceration, distant metastasis.

**Cats**: 80–90% of MGTs malignant (adenocarcinomas most common). Tumors >3 cm: MST 4–12 months.

**Surgery**
- Dogs: lumpectomy acceptable for small (<1 cm) superficial nodules. MGTs 1–2 cm: locoregional mastectomy. Multiple affected glands: chain mastectomy or individual. OHE recommended concurrent.
- Cats: complete unilateral or bilateral mastectomy including underlying fascia "en bloc." Bilateral mastectomy (staged): median survival 1140 days vs. 473 days (unilateral) in multi-institutional study.

**Chemotherapy**
- Dogs: doxorubicin, mitoxantrone, carboplatin used (limited high-quality evidence). Paclitaxel (new formulations) showed efficacy in macroscopic setting.
- IMGC treatment: piroxicam + toceranib + thalidomide ± hypofractionated RT showed improved time to progression vs. medical therapy alone.
- Cats: adjuvant chemotherapy recommended given high malignancy rate; doxorubicin most commonly reported (moderate response ~40–50% macroscopic). Metronomic cyclophosphamide + meloxicam: some benefit in metastatic disease.

### Ch 330 — Urogenital Tumors

**ClinIQ: ✗ MISSING (candidate: DIS-NEO-TCC — Urothelial/Transitional Cell Carcinoma).**

**Transitional Cell Carcinoma (TCC / Invasive Urothelial Carcinoma, iUC)**

**Risk factors (dogs)**
- Age 9–11 years; female-to-male ratio 1.71–1.95:1.
- Scottish Terriers: **21× higher risk** than mixed breeds.
- West Highland White Terriers, Shetland Sheepdogs, Beagles: 3–5× higher risk.
- Obesity, neutering (~2× higher in spayed/neutered), prior cyclophosphamide.
- Older-generation flea products (not newer fipronil spot-ons).
- Vegetable consumption reduces risk in Scottish Terriers.
- Most tumors: intermediate-to-high-grade, trigonal location with urethral/prostatic involvement.
- Metastasis at diagnosis: 10–20%; at necropsy: up to 58%. Sites: LNs, lung, bone, skin.
- Feline TCC: uncommon; often NOT in trigone (27%); metastatic rate 20%.

**BRAF mutation test**
- **BRAF V595E mutation** found in **>80% of dogs with TCC** (also prostatic carcinoma); sensitivity >85%, specificity **100%** in free-catch urine.
- Tool for diagnosis, screening high-risk breeds, monitoring disease progression/treatment response.
- US-guided aspiration/biopsy of bladder NOT recommended due to tumor seeding risk.
- Traumatic catheterization: diagnostic in ~96% females, ~65% males.

**Treatment algorithm (Figure 330.3)**
- Trigonal/urethral involvement (majority) → systemic therapy (NSAID + chemotherapy).
- Surgery for cranially located tumors when possible; TCC can seed abdominal wall during surgery (replaces instruments/gloves after tumor contact).

**Medical treatment**
- Single-agent: ~20% remission rate (NSAID or chemo alone); 35–50% when NSAID combined with chemotherapy.
- SD is most common outcome; cure rare.
- Piroxicam: **0.3 mg/kg PO q24h** (non-selective COX inhibitor, GI effects ~30%); or deracoxib/firocoxib.
- Chemotherapy drugs: vinblastine, carboplatin, mitoxantrone, doxorubicin, gemcitabine (alone or ± NSAID).
- Cisplatin: rarely used due to high toxicity.
- Combination protocols: overall tumor control rate 75%; MSTs >1 year.
- Single-agent MST: ~130–220 days.
- TOC alone: 80% SD, 6% PR; with vinblastine + COX inhibitor: not better than standard vinblastine protocols.
- Metronomic chlorambucil (4 mg/m²/24h): rescue option.

**Palliative/obstructive management**: urethral/ureteral stenting (solves obstruction in 97%; urinary incontinence 26–39%); laser ablation; prepubic cystostomy tubes; RT (61% ORR with low-dose palliative RT + chemo).

**Prostatic carcinoma (PC)**
- Neutered dogs at higher risk (castration not a cause but promotes progression).
- Most are TCC or adenocarcinoma from glandular epithelium/ducts/prostatic urethra.
- Skeletal metastasis in ~40% dogs; common site: ventral aspect of lumbar vertebrae, pelvis, femur.
- BRAF V595E test: applies to urothelial and prostatic carcinoma.
- Treatment largely palliative; COX-2 inhibitor (piroxicam or carprofen) associated with increased survival; IMRT with 54–58 Gy (daily 2.5–2.9 Gy): MST 563–654 days, event-free survival 220–317 days.

**Transmissible venereal tumor (TVT)**
- Vincristine: **0.5–0.7 mg/m² IV weekly**; complete remission 90% after 2–8 injections. Continue 1–2 doses beyond visible resolution. Resistant lesions: doxorubicin or RT.
